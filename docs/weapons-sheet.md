## Why the hammer's two drawings did not match, and the rule from now on

The two candidates were each made from his sword drawing with the words "a hammer instead of a sword", so each one
invented its own hammer (one a glowing blue stone, one grey with an iron band), and each began with a frame of the
sword.

**The rule:** a weapon's look is settled once, as one picture of him holding it (step 2). Every drawing of that
weapon is then made from that picture, so the weapon is the same in every frame. A strip in which the weapon
changes shape, size or colour is thrown away, not trimmed.

**"Every frame that's necessary":** with the second weapon in hand he has nine drawings of his own today (standing,
running, in the air, crouched, crouch-walking, three blows, the plunge). In everything else (the roll, being hurt,
landing, getting up) he is drawn with the sword. A whole set is about sixteen. Where both hands are busy (climbing,
hanging from an edge, swimming) I propose the weapon is on his back for every weapon, drawn once.

**The hammer's look** is the first thing in step 2. You named two ways: the first picture's (a pale stone head that
glows blue) or the second's (grey stone with an iron band across it). I will bring one picture of each, him standing,
and you pick.

### A use in the world for each (for later, with the map pass)

The hammer breaks cracked stone. The others could each open something too: the clapper rings a bell from a
distance (the Cloister's doors open on a bell's beat), the tooth's bounce crosses spikes, the fork lights a far lamp
or throws a far lever, the thorns cut briar. I would not build these until the weapons themselves are right.

## What it costs

About sixteen drawings a weapon, five weapons with the hammer: about eighty strips. There are about 8,900
generations, so it is affordable, with room for strips that are thrown away.
