# 0008 — Landscape, PC first

**Context.** The game was built portrait-first for phones: the play column was capped at 600 px wide, the touch pad took the bottom quarter, and the camera sat about 10 units from the warrior across a shaft of radius 7. Mark wants PC (1920×1080) as the main platform and a camera pulled much further out, growing the world if that is what it takes.

**Decision.** Landscape everywhere, one orientation: PC is the main target, phones play on their side (as Dead Cells does). The page fills the window; the HUD sits in the corners; the touch pad appears only on touch screens, laid over the bottom corners; a phone held upright is asked to turn. The shaft grows from radius 7 to 11 (wall tiling scales with it), so the camera sits well back across the open centre (about 17.5 units from the warrior instead of about 10) with a 50° field of view and thinner fog. One turn of the spiral is now about seven flights instead of four and a half, so the stair sweeps gently across the wide frame. Small screens bring the camera in (to about 62% of the distance at 420 px tall). The internal render height is 720 lines (was about 600), keeping the chunky pixel look. Chains in the open centre are hidden when they would cross the line from the camera to the warrior.

**Consequences.** Less of the shaft above is in view, so threats from above (rockfall, flitters) lean on their tells. The warrior is smaller on screen; lighting and the rim light carry his read. Everything placed by radius was already written relative to R; the tools and tests that assumed 7 were fixed (the test bot's spitter answer, the turn-length test, the art-review camera). Still to come for PC: gamepad support, key rebinding and on-screen prompts that match the device, a render-scale setting, and higher-resolution wall textures for 1080p.
