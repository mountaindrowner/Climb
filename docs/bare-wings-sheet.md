# Three wings with nothing to work: short sheets

*Your word, 26 Nov: "Each wing should feel interesting, contextual, complex. Also we should probably have gates and
lever kind of functions that require the players to explore." (Roadmap, decisions 0.32 and 0.33.) Of the twelve
wings, three have neither a gate he sets nor a rule that acts as one: the Ossuary, the Scriptorium and the Pilgrims'
Cairns. This is one short sheet for each. Nothing here is built yet. Approved 27 Nov ("They all look great"): to be built after the Drowned Galleries.*

The rule for all three, from decision 0.33: **the thing he works is not beside the thing it opens,** and in each
place it is a thing of that place. None of them is a windlass.

---

## 1. The Ossuary (the Roots, east): the scales

**What the place is.** The house laid its dead here. Sextons were sent down to sort them and count them, and they are
counting still. He comes in with the Warden's hammer.

**Its thing: the sextons weigh what they count.** A door in the Ossuary is a grave-slab hung on a chain. The chain
goes up into the roof and comes down somewhere else, to the pan of a scale. Weight in the pan, and the slab goes up.

- **His own weight works,** while he stands in the pan. He steps off, and the slab comes down again. So he can see
  what the pan does, and cannot use it alone.
- **Over each pan, on a ledge, is a stack of the sorted** (skulls, long bones: the heaps already in the wing). Struck
  with the hammer, it goes over into the pan and stays there. The slab stays up.
- **So the work is finding the way up to the ledge over the pan,** which is never the way he came to the pan.
- **The Tallymen take things out of pans.** A Tallyman that reaches a full pan begins carrying it back to its ledge,
  a piece at a time: the slab comes down in steps. Kill it, or be quick.

**Three scales:** one for the Sexton's yard (the wing's fight is behind it), one for the room of his count (the
relic), one that opens the deadhouse stair from this side early.

**Why it is this place's own:** it is sorting and counting made into a door, and it wants the hammer he came in with.

**Cost:** one build. New in the engine: a pan that answers to weight, a stack that falls and stays, a slab that goes up
by degrees. The Tallymen need one new thing to do.

### As built: build 113

*Pictures: `docs/ossuary-scales-build113.png`.*

- **Two scales, not three.**
  - **The yard's:** its slab stands in the way from the niches to the Sexton's yard. Its pan is in the sorting room,
    on the low heap; its stack is on the lip of the shelf over the pan.
  - **The count's:** its slab lies across the hole from the yard up to the count. Its pan is in the niches, on the
    third shelf; its stack is in the niche over it.
- **A slab goes up by as much as lies in its pan.** Three pieces in a stack; the upright slab rises a third a piece.
  The one that lies across a hole lifts only for all three.
- **His own weight lifts it all the way** while he stands in the pan, and it comes down when he steps off.
- **The stack, struck, goes over into the pan and stays.** Any blow does it, not only the hammer's.
- **A Tallyman at a pan with bones in it takes one back every two and a half seconds.** The bones go back to the
  ledge, so the stack can be knocked down again. One Tallyman keeps the sorting room's pan; none keeps the other.
- **His save keeps what lies in each pan.**

**Where it differs from the sheet above**

| The sheet said | It is | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Three scales | **Two** | The third was to open the deadhouse stair "from this side early". That stair is the way out, at the far end: there is no early side to open it from |
| Struck with the hammer | **Any blow** | He may be carrying the sword when he gets there. Making him change weapon to knock over bones is a rule with no reason in it |
| A Tallyman carries a piece back to the ledge | **The piece is gone from the pan and back on the ledge at once** | He stands at the pan for it; he is not seen carrying |

## 2. The Scriptorium (the Cloister, west): the rolling ladders

**What the place is.** Where the house's books were copied. No page may have a fault, so the copyists have been
copying the same first page ever since. He comes in with the Bell's Step, through vellum.

**Its thing: the ladders of the stacks.** The shelves go up out of reach, and the ladders that served them run on a
rail along each wall. A ladder is where the last copyist left it.

- **Struck, a ladder rolls** along its rail until the rail ends or something stops it, and stays there.
- **Where it stops, it is the way up:** its rungs are planks he climbs.
- **The rail goes where he cannot follow it:** through a slot in a wall, or along the far side of a tier of desks. So
  he sends the ladder and then has to find where it went.
- **A ladder can be sent back.** Two of the rails have two places worth reaching, so where he leaves a ladder is a
  choice.
- **A sheet of vellum stops a ladder.** He has to tear it first (the Bell's Step), from the right side.

**Three ladders:** in the copying hall (up to the top tier, where the way on is), in the red room (over the
Corrector's head, to a thread), and in the stacks (a way back from the lectern that does not use the chute).

**Why it is this place's own:** a library's ladder is the only machine a scriptorium has, and it makes the hall's
tiers something to be worked out instead of only climbed.

**Cost:** one build. The engine has things that move and can be stood on (the Orrery Wing's): a ladder is one that
moves only when struck, between stops. His save keeps where each stands.

## 3. The Pilgrims' Cairns (the Spires, west): the wind-breaks

**What the place is.** Where pilgrims who turned back built a cairn before they went down. He comes in with the
Seated's Wind, gliding in rising air.

**Its thing: the walls they slept behind.** On the mountain's shoulder the pilgrims stacked slates into low walls to
keep the wind off. Each wall still stands across the mouth of a gully, and behind it the air is dead.

- **Knock a wall down** (three blows) and the wind goes through. The gully behind it becomes rising air, all the way
  to its far end.
- **The far end is somewhere else:** the gully runs under the field or behind the slope, and comes out where he has
  already been and could not go on.
- **It does not go back up.** A wall knocked down is down for good, as his own cairn is.
- **One wall is a choice.** Two gullies share one wind. Open the second and the first loses half its lift: enough to
  cross, not enough to climb. He decides which he wants more.
- **The cairns' own rule stays as it is** (they move when he is not looking; the stones of the way go if he turns).

**Three walls:** one that makes the way to his own cairn shorter, one that lifts him to a thread on the needle of
rock without the long way round, and the pair that share a wind.

**Why it is this place's own:** everything here was left by people sheltering from the mountain, and the gift that
lets him in is the wind.

**Cost:** one build. The engine has rising air. New: air that begins only when its wall is down, a wall that takes
blows and stays down in his save, and two airs that share strength.

---

### As built: build 114

*Pictures: `docs/cairns-windbreaks-build114.png`.*

- **Two walls, not three, and no shared wind.**
  - **The horn's:** a wall of slates at the far end of the reasons, where the way of stones ends. Down, its wind runs
    back the length of that cave and up the shaft he came down by, to the head of the horn of rock at the field's
    west end. **A thread is on the horn's head**, seventeen tiles up a sheer face: with the wall standing it is out
    of reach of everything he has.
  - **The scree's:** a wall on the scree's bank. Down, its wind goes up the hole to the slope, so the scree is climbed
    from below. That makes the wing's way out a way in as well, from Ordric's hollow.
- **Three blows bring a wall down. It stays down,** and his save keeps it.
- **Behind a wall that stands, nothing shows:** no streaks in the air.

**Where it differs from the sheet above**

| The sheet said | It is | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Three walls | **Two** | I built three first. A test showed they changed nothing: he leaps and hauls himself up fourteen tiles and a half, and glides a long way, so everything was in reach already. Only a sheer height of sixteen or more needs a wind. There were two such places |
| A wind that lifts him over the Turned | **Not built** | It would make the wing's fight something to float past |
| Two gullies that share one wind, so opening the second halves the first | **Not built** | A wall cannot be put back. Opening the wrong one first would lose him a thread for good |
| A thread on the needle of rock | **On the horn** at the field's west end, made taller | The needle is eleven tiles: he climbs that. It keeps its own wind, as before |
