# Phase 16: the Flooded Levels, the Great Shaft, and travel through the water (the sheet)

*The plan was written before building; "As built" at the end says what came of it.*

Three things, and one rule under all of them.

## The rule: the water bears him, and with the wind it carries him

Until now water has been a basin he stands in. Here it is deep.

- **Without the Wyrm's wind,** deep water bears him up. He floats at the chest, can swim along the top, and can climb
  out. He cannot go under. No water in the game drowns him: it is the water that gives him back.
- **With the wind,** the thread opens under the water as it does in rising air, and carries him. Hold **jump** to
  rise, hold **down** to sink, left and right to go along; let go and he hangs where he is, sinking a very little.
  He can strike under water. At the surface, jump takes him out.
- **No breath.** The plan's "air-pockets" are rooms, not a meter. The fear here is the water over his head; what he
  finds is that it holds him. A count running down would say the opposite.
- **The lamp** still lights under water, a little less far. The guild's lamps that went under are still lit, faintly
  and green: under the water is the lit part of these levels, and the air above it is the dark part.

So the one gate of this phase is everywhere the same: **going under needs the wind.**

## The Flooded Levels

*The Deep, under the Workings' cistern. Twelve screens. Dark. Needs the Wyrm's wind.*

At six hundred metres the guild cut into water. It was the same water as the pool above. It took four levels in a
night. They built a pump to drive it out, and went on digging underneath it.

**The way in.** In the floor of the Workings' cistern, under its water, a sump: a square hole with a green light a
long way down it. Without the wind he floats over it. With it, he goes down.

**Rooms** (it goes down, as the Deep does):

1. **The sump** (two screens, one over the other). The drowned shaft. He comes in at the top, under water, and goes
   down past three of the guild's lamps, still lit. At its foot the shaft turns and comes up into **the first air**:
   a dry chamber, the level's raising water (a stand-pipe the guild drove into the flood), and **Hob**, who has come
   down ahead by a way he will not describe.
2. **The air-pockets** (two screens). Domes of trapped air, one after another, the water standing in the floor of
   each, joined under their walls. **Rafts** of the guild's rubbish float in them: they hold him for a moment and
   then go down. **The Sunk** lie under the rafts. A thread on a high shelf, reached from a raft before it sinks.
3. **The drowned level** (three screens: the vast one). A whole gallery under water to its roof: the rail, the carts,
   a lamp every few yards still lit. **Blind eels** in the wall. **The Sunk** on the floor between the rails.
4. **The chapel of the shift** (one screen). A niche the miners carved, flooded to the altar. A guild lamp on the
   altar, burning under the water. Nothing guards it. **Here the water teaches him to travel** (below).
5. **The pump-house** (two screens). **The Pump**, the level's keeper.
6. **The overflow** (two screens; opens when the Pump runs free). Where the water pours down a stair into the dark.
   The relic. At its foot, a way down into the Vein, beside the guild's seal: the second way into the Vein, and a
   loop.

**Who is here.**

- **The Sunk.** Pale shapes that were miners, lying on the bottom. When he passes over one it rises at him, slowly,
  arms first. It cannot leave the water. Three blows.
- **Blind eels.** In holes in the wall. They cannot see him. They feel the water move: if he goes fast near one it
  strikes, straight and very quick, the length of the gallery, and goes back. If he lets the water carry him slowly
  past, it never knows. Two blows.
- **Rafts.** Not creatures. Planks and a barrel. Stood on, a raft sinks under him; left, it comes back up.
- **The Pump** (keeper; drawn in code, in pieces). An iron beam-engine that has worked for two hundred years. It
  cannot be killed: it is fought by its three **valves**, wheels on its body at three heights.
  - Each stroke of the beam **moves the water in the room**: down to the floor, then up to the roof, in a slow tide.
  - **At low water** the floor is dry, the low valve can be struck, and its **pistons** come down on where he stands
    (a warning, then the blow).
  - **At high water** he swims to the middle and high valves. The intake draws **the Sunk** in, one at a time.
  - With each valve broken the stroke quickens.
  - When the third valve breaks **it does not stop.** It runs free. The water goes out of the room for good, down the
    overflow, and the grate over the overflow opens. The banner: *The Pump runs free. It has stopped nothing.*

**Light.** Cold green under the water, from the drowned lamps. Above it, in the pockets, only his lamp and the guild's
lamps he lights.

**Underneath.** Being overwhelmed: the water over his head. And the water holds him. Nobody says so.

**The relic** (in the overflow): **the Shift Bell**, the small bell that called the miners up and was never rung that
night. *Under water, he goes faster, and the eels do not feel him.* (Something particular to this place, not a
number on his hearts.)

**Hob's lines here:** that the pump was his grandfather's; that it has never been behind and never been ahead; that
the things in the water were on his shift.

## The Great Shaft

*The Deep, below the Vein's lip. Four screens. Dark. Needs the Wyrm's wind to finish.*

The Auger's hole. It bored straight down for seven hundred metres and broke through into something.

- **The drop** (three screens, one over another). From the lip, nothing. The guild's last camp hangs on ropes down
  the walls: a stage, a cot, a kettle on a hook, a ladder of planks from one to the next, so it can be climbed
  either way. Their marks on the wall: a depth and a date; lower, only a depth. **Cold air comes up it.** Under the
  scarf he comes down it slowly, the longest glide in the game, and nothing in it but him.
- **At its foot, water.** Black, and deep. Whoever falls the shaft falls into it and is unhurt.
  - Without the wind he floats there, and the camp's lowest ladder is the way out: *a fall into the water, and the
    climb again*, as the plan says.
  - With the wind he goes under, through a drowned arch, and comes up in:
- **The floor** (one screen). Dry rock, and in the middle of it the hole the Auger went on through. **A warm light
  comes up out of it.** It is not his lamp and it is not lampstone. The way down is for the next phase.

**Who is here.** Nobody. A raising water at the head of the drop (the camp's water-butt), a thread in the camp, and the
level's relic on the floor: **the Last Mark**, the guild's chalk. *The map shows what he has not found in a room he
is standing in.* (For Corwen's map: the room's unfound threads, chests and relics are marked while he is in it.)

Hob is not here. He does not go down the Shaft, and says so in the Vein.

## Travel through the water

The plan (the Metroidvania notes, idea 1): the water is the fast travel, and it is given late, where the story gives
the reason for it.

- **Where it is given:** the chapel of the shift, at the lamp burning under the water. He comes to it, the page
  opens: *The water knows him. At any water he has found, he can go under, and come up at another.*
  It is before the Pump, not after, so nobody is kept from it by a fight.
- **How it is used:** at any raising water or leak, the prompt that opens the knots now opens **the water**: the knots
  as before, and under them **Go under**. That opens a list of every water he has touched, by region, the raising
  waters first. Choose one: he comes apart into the water as he does in a fall, and rises out of the other. No word
  over it; it is not a fall, and it is not counted as one.
- **What counts as found:** a water he has stood in since this build. A save from before it has the raising water of
  every region it has reached; leaks are found again by walking to them.
- **It does not** refill the creatures of the place he leaves or bring a fallen keeper back; it is travel, not rest.

## What is checked

- Without the wind he cannot reach the sump's passage, the floor of the Shaft, or anything under deep water; with it,
  every room of both levels, and back out again.
- Nowhere in the water can he be stuck, with or without the wind.
- The Sunk stay in the water. The eel strikes a fast swimmer and not a slow one, and not one who carries the Shift
  Bell. The Pump's tide, its valves, and that the overflow is shut until it runs free.
- Travel: only waters he has found; from each to each; the save keeps them; an old save gets its raising waters.
- The usual: each level's relic, two threads, Hob (not in the Shaft, which is noted), its page in the Annals, its
  voices, every marker in open air, every runback.

## For the player to try (the playtest list)

1. In the Workings' cistern **before the Wyrm**: wade in. Does floating read as "not yet", or as a bug?
2. With the wind: go under at the sump. Does the first moment under water feel like being carried, or like losing
   control? Is rising and sinking the right speed?
3. Drift past an eel slowly; then rush one. Is it clear why it struck?
4. The Pump: is the tide readable? Is there always something to do at low water and at high?
5. The chapel: is it clear what was given, and where to use it?
6. Go under at one water and come up at another, three or four times across the mountain. Does it feel earned, and
   is the list quick enough to use?
7. The Shaft under the scarf: is the long glide quiet and good, or just long?

## As built

*Build 73.* Built as above, with these differences and details.

- **The Workings' cistern was rebuilt for it.** It had a basin he waded in. It is now deep: the water stands to four
  times his depth across the whole room, he crosses on the stones or along its top, and the sump is in the floor
  under it with a green lamp at its mouth. The guild's seal room, east of it, stands on a quay at the water's height,
  and its stair to the Vein goes down through its floor. Without the wind the first time: *The water bears him up.
  He cannot go under it. Not yet.* With it: *The water carries him.*
- **Swimming.** With the wind he goes at about two thirds of his run, rises and sinks a little faster than that, and
  let go he sinks very slowly. Without it he is held at the chest. At the top, jump is a leap out, enough to reach a
  bank level with the water, and a little more. He does not double jump or glide out of water. Under water he lies
  along the way he is going, and his lamp is greener and reaches four fifths as far.
- **The rooms** are as planned, twelve screens. The chapel and the drowned level are under water to their roofs. The
  sump's first air has the raising water (a stand-pipe) and Hob.
- **The Pump's hall and the water over it.** A room that fills and empties under a level that is always full needed
  a reason to hold together: the way in is a trunk that is always full, then under a wall into a trench that is
  always full, and the hall's floor stands above the trench. Low water is the trench; high water is the hall to its
  roof. Its three valves are wheels at the floor, on its body and near the roof. Two of the Sunk lie in the trench and
  are drawn in one at a time at high water; they do not rise for someone only swimming past.
- **The overflow** is a stair of twelve steps down to the west, a leak half way down it, the Shift Bell at its foot,
  and the way down into the Vein beside the guild's seal. That way is one way: he comes back by the seal's stair.
- **The Great Shaft** is three screens of drop and one of floor. The camp is eight stages, a jump apart, either way.
  From the head to the water under the scarf is about seventeen seconds. Its floor's relic is **the Last Rope** (he
  pulls himself up onto an edge twice as fast), not the Last Mark: the mountain already has a relic that marks the
  map (the Astronomer's Compass).
- **Travel.** As planned, with one difference: going under makes the place he comes up in afresh, as walking into a
  region does, so its creatures are there again. It does not bring a fallen keeper back, and it is not counted as a
  fall. A save from before this build is given the raising water of every region it has been in.
- **Art.** The Sunk are drawn (one drawing, six cents). The eel, the valves, the Pump, the rafts, the lamps under
  water and the water itself are drawn in code.

**What is weaker than the sheet.**

- The Pump's upper valves can be reached at low water by a double jump and a cut in the air. The tide is the easier
  way to them, not the only one.
- The overflow's water is painted on its stair; it does not run or push.
- The rafts matter less than I meant: with the wind he can swim under them. They are the dry way across, and the way
  up to one thread.
- Nothing here has been played by a person. How swimming feels is the thing this phase most needs you for.
