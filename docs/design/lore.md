# Lore

Background for The Climb, for the people making it. The player gets it only in pieces: an item's two sentences, a plaque, a statue's turned head. Every region is an age of history, stacked in order. The bottom is the oldest catastrophe, the top the most recent. Climbing is moving forward in time.

This builds on `direction.md` and `story.md`. The designer's region list replaces the earlier seven working zones; their best ideas live on inside it (rising water in Gutterlight, forges in Haldmarrow, spore pods in Crookwarren). The mountain is **Orrow**. The spiral stair is **the Wending**, which replaces the placeholder "The Long Stair".

## The one-paragraph truth

> **Hidden from the player.** Pieced together only near the summit, and never stated outright.

At the summit of Orrow, under the open sky, the warrior once knelt beside someone he could not save, and he refused to let it end. The mountain took him at his word. His refusal became water, and water runs down: it ran down through the stone and back through the years, until it pooled at the very bottom of the mountain and the very beginning of its history. So the Pool is older than anything in Orrow and younger than the man who made it. Every age that touched it learned that nothing had to end, and every age fell of that lesson. The first things in the stone drank longest. They lost their shapes and their names, and they crowd the bottom because the Pool is the only mother they have known. The warrior cannot die because he is the wish, and a wish does not end until its maker lets it. For the same reason his blade is the only thing in the mountain that truly ends what it strikes, and his hands the only ones whose repairs stay made. At the summit the moment is still held open. The dawn has not broken. The snow hangs in the air. The one he could not save has been kept so long that they have stilled to stone and become the last Warden. What waits at the top is the choice he refused the first time: to let go.

## The warrior

He wakes in the Pool with no name. That is not a device; it is a cost. Names wear away under the Pool, and he has been under it a long time.

What he has says more than he does. A mail shirt. A banded shield. A nose-guard helm. A lantern on his hip, its hood dented on one side. The lantern is the clearest clue, and it is not explained until Knellspire, where the Lantern Watch kept hooks for lamps exactly like it.

He does not speak. He does not need a reason to climb, because the player supplies one: up is where the light is. Let him have only gestures. He touches the wall where tally marks are cut. He stops, once, to look at the sky in Whitherwind. Everything else is the player's.

> *Lantern, dented.* An iron lantern of plain make, the hood knocked in on one side. It burns without oil. You do not remember lighting it.

## The Pool

**What it does.** It returns. Whatever falls into it comes back up whole, breathing, flask brimming. It does not heal so much as refuse. The flask is filled from it, which is why a rest stop can sell a mouthful but only the source can fill it.

**What it keeps.** Everything falls to the Pool in the end. Gold thrown down the shaft waits at its edge. This is a Hald custom, the *casting of the tithe*: the dwarves threw gold to the water in the belief that it bought their return. The pouch does not always survive. The longer the fall, the more things in the dark have a chance to reach for it.

**What it costs.** Two things, slowly.

- *Names.* Each return wears a little away. The things at the bottom have none left. The warrior has already lost his.
- *Motion.* What the Pool keeps and does not move, it stills. Stone creeps in from the heart outward, and the teal light settles in the chest where the heart was. Every Warden is a stilled thing. The warrior is spared only because he does not stop. *Keep climbing, or become a Warden.*

**Who made it.** Every age had its answer. The Wickfolk said it was a lamp lit under the world. The Hald said it was the mountain's blood. Haverel said it was a resource. The Lantern Watch came closest: they called it *the Welling*, and wrote that it was "someone's unwillingness." The truth is above.

**Why he falls.** Death on the Wending is not death. It is the Pool calling its maker home. A Warden that tears the stair from under him is not trying to kill him. Nothing here can. It is sending him back down.

## Region by region

Each Warden guards the border between its age and the next. It cannot bear what comes after, so it keeps the stair closed. That is why the dark above an unbeaten Warden hides everything: the future is not visible until the past has ended. When the warrior ends a Warden, its age is finally over, the dark lifts, and it stays over.

### 1. The Deep: **the Lowmost**

*The Unnumbered Age. Before counting. Before names.*

**What happened.** Nothing anyone wrote down. Blind things lived in the young stone, slow and many-legged and hungry. Then the water came seeping from above and pooled here, and they drank first. They could not die. Ages passed over them like weather. They became what is left.

**Who lives here now.** Crawlers, spitters, burrowers, flitters: the Pool's oldest children. They sleep in the rock where its water seeps, and they wake at the smell of it. The warrior smells of nothing else. Behind the wall, too large to see whole, are the Unnumbered, the first of all, which barely move. Below the Pool is something even the Wardens will not look at.

**Warden: Urrow, the First Kept.** The first thing ever to climb out of the Pool and stand up. It learned to stand. It forgot why. Its name survives only as worn letters on the wall behind it.
*Why it guards:* It was here first, and it does not know there is an after.

**Found here.**
> *Flask of the Welling.* Teal water that closes wounds and asks nothing in return. Be wary of anything that asks nothing.

> *Scratched letters.* Cut deep beside a crack: U, R, R, then a smooth patch where the stone has been rubbed by something that came back to touch it, again and again.

**Seen, not read.** A ring of crawler husks lies around the Pool's edge, every one turned to face the water, like a congregation.

**Hook.** The crack glows before it opens because the glow is the Pool's own water seeping through. Wherever the water seeps, something drinks. This is the fiction behind the built spawn tell: a glowing crack always means a waking thing.

### 2. The Mid Caverns: **Gutterlight**

*The Vigil. The first people.*

**What happened.** The Wickfolk were small, pale cave people who lived by candle. They found the glow far below and called it the Lamp Under the World. They cut the first steps of the Wending to go down and keep vigil over it. Then their dead began to walk back up out of the water. At first they called it the Returning and wept for joy. Each time, less came back. So they walled the returned into the lower halls, and flooded those halls, and the vigil became a guard. Then the candles ran out.

**Who lives here now.** *The Guttered*: wax-pale shapes that rise from the black water where it touches the stair. Moths that come to the warrior's lantern because there is no other light left to come to.

**Warden: Mother Tallowen.** The last keeper of the vigil. She drank so she could keep watch forever, and the last candle still burns in her chest, in wax gone to stone.
*Why she guards:* She swore no one would go down to the Lamp again, and she has not noticed that you are coming up.

**Found here.**
> *Tallow stub.* The Wickfolk burned one for each of their dead. Then the dead came back, and there were not enough candles. Then there were too many dead.

> *Votive figure.* Clay, its face rubbed smooth by thumbs. Pressed into the back, hard: *Stay down, love. Stay down.*

**Seen, not read.** Below the waterline, bricked arches. In the mortar, handprints, pressed from the inside.

**Hook.** The water rises behind you. The Wickfolk's floodgates are failing, and the black water climbs the stair at your back. Where it touches a step, the Guttered rise.

### 3. The Dwarf Mines: **the Wellseams**

*The Delving.*

**What happened.** The Hald came down from the high halls, following seams of *wellstone*: teal rock where the Pool's water had soaked into the mountain over ages. Wellstone tools never dulled. Miners who breathed its dust healed overnight and never tired. So they dug faster and deeper, and broke through into Gutterlight's sealed halls, and through those into the Lowmost. They built the first lifts to haul the stone up.

**Who lives here now.** *The Shift*: stone-skinned miners still swinging picks at seams long since empty. They do not hate you. You are only in the way of the work.

**Warden: Delvemaster Grist.** The foreman of the Ninth Seam. His shift never ended, so he never left it.
*Why he guards:* No one rang the bell to end the shift, and he is still waiting to hear it.

**Found here.**
> *War pick.* A Hald pick with a head of wellstone and a haft worn to the shape of one grip. It does not dull. The hand that held it did not tire. That was the trouble.

> *Lift plate.* Cast into the cage: *BUILT BY THE HALD TO RISE.* Scratched beneath it in another hand: *and to bring them down.*

**Seen, not read.** An ore cart tipped at the shaft's edge, its load of teal stone spilling into the dark. A miner still holds the brake. He is stone, and his chest is glowing.

**Hook.** Listen for picks. The Shift is digging somewhere above you, and rockfall comes down where the picking stops. The Hald ore rails spiral the shaft here too: the fiction for the proposed minecart ride.

### 4. The Dwarf Empire: **Haldmarrow**

*The Dominion. The Hald at their height.*

**What happened.** Built on wellstone, Haldmarrow never aged. Its forges never went out. Its people never died, and in time they stopped having children, because nobody ever left to make room. The oldest began to *settle*: to turn to stone a little each year, the teal light showing through. The Hald called it an honor. They lined their avenues with the settled and called them statues. For those who settled too slowly to work, they cast iron shells.

**Who lives here now.** *The Moldborn*: iron shells that step out of the casting molds when the great bellows breathe. Most are empty now. The settled line the stair, and some of them still turn their heads.

**Warden: Hessa of the Unbanked Forge.** High smith of the Dominion. She has tended the great forge for an age, and is now half iron and half stone, with molten seams at the joints.
*Why she guards:* If the fire ever goes out, someone will have to say the empire is over.

**Found here.**
> *Iron cradle.* Wrought with great care and never used. The Hald stopped making children in the third century of the Dominion. No one could remember why they had wanted them.

> *Whetstone.* Haldmarrow wellstone, cut for edges. A blade kissed by it bites deeper for a while. Only for a while. Even the Hald could not make that last.

**Seen, not read.** An avenue of settled Hald, each with a dim glow in its chest. They all face the stair. Each time you pass, one of them has turned a little further.

**Hook.** The forge breathes. On each exhale of the bellows, heat vents fire across the stair and the molds open.

### 5. The Dwarf Throne: **the Abiding Seat**

*The Stilling.*

**What happened.** King Ormagund was the first to drink the water itself on purpose. He sent a miner down the Wellseams with a tin cup. He drank. He did not die. When the deep things came up through the broken seams, he did not flee and he did not fight. He sealed the lower halls, barred the throne doors, and commanded his court to stand fast. They are still standing.

**Who lives here now.** *The Stillguard*, the king's honor guard, stone to the marrow. They move on his heartbeat and only then.

**Warden: Ormagund the Abiding.** The king, the crown grown into his skull like roots through stone.
*Why he guards:* He promised his people the Dominion would never end, and he keeps his promises.

**Found here.**
> *Royal decree.* *By the Abiding King: let no one leave the Seat. Let no one leave the Seat. Let no one leave the Seat.* It goes on to the edge of the slate, and around the back.

> *Tin cup.* Dented. A miner's. It carried the king's first drink up from the deep. The miner was given a title, and his is the only name chiseled off the hall.

**Seen, not read.** A feast hall of stone Hald seated at long tables, the food gone to dust. One chair is empty and pushed back. Someone stood up and left.

**Hook.** The heartbeat. You hear it through the stone, slow and loud. The Stillguard step, swing and close on the beat, and so do the Warden's tells: the place to sync threats to a rhythm.

### 6. The Dragon's Roost: **Hearthroost**

*The Burning.*

**What happened.** Caldrith, the last of her kind, came out of the sky carrying a clutch. She smelled the Pool from far off: the one place in the world where eggs would never die. She burned her way into the mountain's flank, through the upper forges the king had sealed off, and laid her eggs in the cooling furnaces. The eggs did not die. They never hatched. She has been waiting a thousand years.

**Who lives here now.** *The Cindered*: the Hald of the upper halls, burned and unable to stop burning. Embers that drift and flare like moths.

**Warden: Caldrith, the Banked Fire.** The dragon herself, coiled behind the wall around her clutch.
*Why she guards:* Everything above her is a world in which her children never hatched.

**Found here.**
> *Eggshell shard.* Warm. It has been warm for a thousand years. Hold it to your ear and something inside turns over, as if in sleep.

> *Furnace plaque.* *FORGE SEVEN. FIRE BY ROYAL GRANT. NEVER TO BE BANKED.* The dragon took that last part to heart.

**Seen, not read.** Seven eggs in a cold furnace, ringed by Hald shields. One egg has been carefully wrapped in a blanket far too crude for a dragon's work.

**Hook.** Her breath comes through the gaps in the wall. The gaps glow orange and the air shimmers first. Get behind a shield stack and crouch, or roll through between breaths.

### 7. The Troll Colony: **Crookwarren**

*The Hunger.*

**What happened.** Outside, the Long White began: a winter that did not end. Trolls from the high slopes followed the warmth in through Caldrith's burn and settled in her hot ash. They grew pale mushroom gardens on the bones of the Hald. Under the Pool's reach they did not die at all. Their young never grew up. The colony swelled past anything the gardens could feed, and the trolls learned that a troll can be hungry for a hundred years.

**Who lives here now.** Trolls: huge, slow, starving, gentle with one another and savage with you. *Gnawlings*: troll young, centuries old, small and fast and always hungry. Spore pods from the gardens, run wild.

**Warden: Gammer Oskun, the Ever-Fed.** The colony mother. When the food ran out she fed her children on herself, and she grew back, and she fed them again.
*Why she guards:* Above her is the cold, and she will not let her children go out into it.

**Found here.**
> *Lullaby bone.* Scratches in rows, which turn out to be a song: *Sleep, sleep, the warm is ours. The cold is long. The warm is ours.*

> *Troll blanket.* Woven from dragon scale and hair. Too small for a troll. Just the right size for an egg.

**Seen, not read.** A nursery of cradles carved from Hald statues, a Gnawling asleep in each. On the wall above them, height marks scratched in the stone. Hundreds of marks. All at the same height.

**Hook.** Trolls hunt by sound in the dark. Crouch-walk past the sleepers. Run, roll or fight near them and they wake. Spore pods burst under a troll's stomp and swallow the light.

### 8. The Lich Castle: **Morrowkeep**

*The Chancery.*

**What happened.** Men came in from the Long White after the trolls. Their lord, Chancellor Haverel, was a careful scholar who feared death. He read the mountain's layers as one reads a ledger, and he understood: the water keeps. He built Morrowkeep in the hollow above the warren and ran iron pipes and Hald chains down the shaft to draw the Welling up, against its fall. Then he sold years. But water drawn upward keeps the body and not the life. Those who drank went on, cold. Haverel drank most of all.

**Who lives here now.** *The Debtors*: the undead who bought years and are still paying them off, chained to their ledgers. *The Bailiffs*, in armor, who collect.

**Warden: Chancellor Haverel.** The lich. He was a man who made a business of fear, and he is now its only stock.
*Why he guards:* Every year above him is one he has not yet sold.

**Found here.**
> *Bill of years.* *The bearer is owed forty years, payable in water.* Signed, sealed, and spotted with something that is not ink. The bearer never collected.

> *Chancellor's spectacles.* Thick lenses for small print. Haverel read the history of the mountain from the bottom up, line by line. He never read the last line.

**Seen, not read.** A hall of cracked pipes dripping teal. Under every drip kneels a Debtor, mouth open, waiting.

**Hook.** Toll gates. Haverel's gates bar the short way up. Pay the toll in gold and they open. Refuse and you take the long way, through the Bailiffs.

### 9. The Bell Tower: **Knellspire** *(new)*

*The Watch.*

**What happened.** Those who would not drink climbed above Morrowkeep and founded the Lantern Watch in the high bell tower. They believed the water was a wish, *someone's unwillingness*, and that a wish must be answered with endings. They rang a knell for every thing that should have died and could not, so that at least it was named and mourned. They sent lanternbearers down the Wending to carry the knell to the bottom. None came back. The bells cracked one by one. By then the water had soaked the whole mountain, and even the Watch could not die.

**Who lives here now.** *The Muffled Brothers*, ringing hand-bells wrapped in cloth. Bells on ropes swing across the stair where the tower's frame has failed: the built swinging weight, recast.

**Warden: Tollan, the Last Ringer.** The old abbot. He began to ring the knell for the whole world, and he cannot stop.
*Why he guards:* The knell is not finished, and no one may pass until it is.

**Found here.**
> *Hand-bell of the Watch.* Bronze, the clapper bound in cloth. The Watch rang these softly beside those who could not die. It did not help. It was not meant to. It meant: we noticed.

> *Lanternbearer's oath.* *I go down with the light. I do not come back with it. What I find, I end. What ends, I name.*

> *Knell roll.* A list of names to be tolled, longer than the tower is tall. The last entries are in a shaking hand. The final line is ruled, and blank.

**Seen, not read.** By the tower gate, a long row of iron hooks for the lanternbearers' lamps, a name plate above each. Every hook is empty. One plate has been pried off.

**Hook.** The toll rolls down the stair as a visible ring: jump it, or brace behind your shield. Between tolls, the Muffled Brothers stand still.

### 10. The Outer Face: **Whitherwind** *(new)*

*The Long White. The present age.*

**What happened.** This is now. The water reached the whole world in the end, and nothing ends anymore. Not even winter. Here the shaft's wall is broken open to the outside, and through the breaches the warrior sees the world for the first time. Pilgrims and refugees came to Orrow in their thousands to climb to the top, where it was said spring was kept. They froze on the way, and they are still alive.

**Who lives here now.** *The Rimebound*: frozen pilgrims who crack free as you pass and climb after you. Carrion kites that circle the breaches and cannot starve.

**Warden: Ossel the Undelivered.** A herald sent up the mountain to ask why spring would not come. He froze climbing and kept climbing, and an age of rime has made a colossus of him. The letter is still in his fist.
*Why he guards:* He was sent to reach the summit first, and he is still on his way.

**Found here.**
> *The herald's letter.* The wax gone white. *To the keepers of the high places: the snow has lain nine hundred years. The children have not grown. We ask for nothing. We only ask why.*

> *Seed pouch.* Seed corn, carried up to plant at the top, where spring was said to be kept. The seeds are whole. They have been waiting too.

**Seen, not read.** Through a breach: the world, white to the horizon. A city with every chimney smoking, and the smoke standing in still columns that never drift.

**Hook.** Wind through the breaches pushes you toward the open center. Snow streamers whip across first. Hold block to brace.

### 11. The Peak: **Skysill** *(new)*

*The Wish. Not an age: a moment.*

**What happened.** Here the warrior knelt and refused. Nothing has moved since. The sun is just below the horizon and the sky is pale gold at its rim: a dawn held for all of time. A great tree grows from a cleft in the summit, and its roots go down into the shaft, all the way down. Under it is a stone bier with someone on it beneath a cloak. Beside them burns a lantern exactly like his.

**Who lives here now.** No enemies. A cairn holds one stone for each Warden the warrior has ended, laid by no hand he saw.

**Warden: the Held.** The one he could not save. Kept from death so long that they have stilled like all the rest, the teal light in their chest the brightest in the mountain. When he comes, they rise.
*Why they guard:* They guard the wish, because the wish is the only thing still keeping them.

**Found here.**
> *The other lantern.* The same make as yours. The same dent in the hood. It has been burning since before the mountain had a bottom.

> *Root-wood.* A splinter of the summit tree. Its roots go down further than anything has climbed up. They catch what falls. They have been reaching for one thing the whole time.

**Seen, not read.** Snowflakes hang motionless around the summit. As the warrior walks, they part around him and stay parted, so his path is carved in the hanging snow behind him.

**Hook.** The only flat, open ground in the game, and the only fight with no stair. No root grows above here, so a fall from the summit is the longest in the game. After the Held falls, the flask is the ending: pour it out on the stone, or drink. (See the open questions.)

## The threads

These run through every region and pay off at the top.

1. **The lantern.** His lantern burns without oil. In every region there are empty niches and hooks where lanterns once hung. At Knellspire the hooks have name plates, and one plate is gone. At Skysill a lantern identical to his is already burning. *Was he a lanternbearer sent down, or did he light the first one?*

2. **The roots.** On every fall a root reaches from the stair's edge, and the player learns to catch it. Higher up the roots are thicker and older. At the summit the player sees where they come from: the tree over the bier. The roots catch him because they are reaching for him. They can only hold so much of him, which is why a caught fall leaves him at a third of his health.

3. **Worn names.** Names are fuller the higher you climb. At the bottom there are none: Urrow's survives as a few scratched letters. The Hald chisel names off. The warrior has none. The knell roll's last line is blank. Whether the summit fills it in is a choice for later (see the open questions).

4. **Fetch.** The trader at every rest stop. His coat changes; he does not. He wears a Wickfolk shawl in Gutterlight, a Hald apron in Haldmarrow, a Watch cloak in Knellspire. He sells single mouthfuls of Pool water at heights where there should be none. He never says how he got down here. A *fetch* is an old word for the double you see just before your death. At the summit he is sitting by the cairn with nothing to sell, and he finally answers: *"Same as you. I came down with you. Someone had to."* One reading: he is the death the warrior refused, and he has been keeping him company ever since.

5. **The tally.** Cut into the wall beside the Pool: four strokes and a slash, over and over, down the stone and under the waterline. The player's fall counter is always smaller than the number of marks. The oldest marks are worn nearly smooth. The hand is always the same. The same marks are cut into the edge of the bier.

## Open questions for the designer

1. **How true is the truth?** Is the warrior really the maker of the Pool, or only the latest to believe he is? The lore supports either. The first is a stronger ending; the second keeps the mystery open for good.
2. **The ending.** Pour the flask out (spring, endings, the warrior's own) or drink (wake in the Pool again, one more tally mark)? One ending, both, or a third: give the flask to the Held?
3. **Names.** Does the player ever learn the warrior's name, or the Held's? Does the knell roll's blank line fill in at the end, does the player write it, or does it stay blank?
4. **Who is Fetch?** The death the warrior refused, the warrior's own double, or the first lanternbearer? His summit line works for all three.
5. **The tally and new game plus.** The marks outnumber the player's falls. Is that a hint at earlier loops that a new game plus makes real, or only atmosphere?
6. **The pursuer.** If something hunts him up the shaft, is it one of the Unnumbered following the smell of the Pool, Urrow refusing to stay ended, or the rising water itself?
7. **Cross-region echoes.** Should actions high up change things lower down? For example, ringing a bell at Knellspire could at last end Grist's shift in the Wellseams.
8. **The warrior and the Held.** The build's warrior is "he". Should the rig offer a choice, and should the Held's form, kept vague here, stay vague forever?
