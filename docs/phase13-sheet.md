# Phase 13: the Gardens' wings (the sheet)

Two wings: the Aqueducts (west of the wellspring and below it, open to anyone) and the Keepers' Houses (east, behind
the Toll's step). This sheet is written wing by wing, each before it is built, and each is closed with what was built
and what was checked.

## The Aqueducts

The garden's water comes from the pool at the bottom of the world. In the first days it was carried up on men's
shoulders; then the house cut a channel through the mountain. It still runs. Stone servants of the house keep the
sluices. Nobody up here remembers where the water comes from. Nine screens.

### Its rule: the water runs one way, and it carries him

- **Along a channel** the water carries whatever stands in it, at six units a second (his run is 7.6). With it, he
  goes at 13.6 and is thrown off the end of a span in a leap of sixteen tiles. Against it, he gains ground slowly.
  Standing, he is taken.
- **Down a fall**, nothing rises: no jump, no glide, no grabbing a ledge. A fall across a climb bars the climb. It
  does not hurt, and being borne down by one is not a fall.
- **A sluice wheel**, struck, stops its water; struck again, it runs. Every wheel stands on dry stone. A wheel stays
  as he left it, and is remembered in his save.
- **The keepers of the sluices** put each wheel back as the house left it.

So the falls are the gates, and the wheels are their keys; the keepers lock them again behind him, or under him.

### How it is entered, and left

- **In:** a door in the wellspring's west wall, at its floor, onto the last span of the channel. The water on that
  span runs toward him: his first steps in the wing are against it. By the door, rungs go down to the beds and
  back up, so nobody is left below.
- **Out:** the same way, fast, on the water. Or, once the Lock is open and the spill's wheel is shut, down the spill
  to a second door high in the wellspring's west wall. From then on the spill is a stair both ways.

### The rooms

1. **The arches** (four screens: the vast space). The channel on its arcade, three spans of it fallen, the old beds
   under it. He comes in on the channel and cannot go west along it against the water and across the gaps without
   work, so the easy way is down into the beds, west under the arches, and up at the far end to the channel's head
   and its wheel. From there the water takes him back east, span to span, in three leaps, past the thread on the
   middle span, and out of the door he came in by.
2. **The sluices** (two screens, one over the other: the winding way). Three falls, one over the other, a wheel
   under each, a keeper on each landing. Stop a fall, climb it, and its keeper starts it again.
3. **The lock** (one screen). The wing's keeper.
4. **The head** (one screen). Where the water comes out of the rock. The relic. The spill's wheel.
5. **The spill** (one screen: the path no one expects). The overflow, down to the wellspring.

### What is in it

- **Sluice-keeper.** A stone servant, slow, six blows to break. It walks its landing. A wheel that is not as the house
  left it, it walks to (faster) and puts back: about three seconds from close by. What stands in front of it, it
  strikes down after a clear wind-up; a guard turned to it stops the blow.
- **Water-strider.** Two blows. On running water it is fast and stings. With its water stopped it sits where it was
  left and does nothing. So a wheel is also a weapon.
- **The Lock** (the wing's keeper; 24 blows). The great gate between the lock room and the head. It cannot be moved
  and does not strike. Again and again it warns for a second (it shakes, its wheel spins, water spits under it) and
  then lets go what it holds down the length of the floor for a second and a half. Whoever stands in that water as
  it comes is swept back and loses a heart. Two piers stand over the water: be up on one. It opens by thirds as it
  is struck, and lets go more often each time (every 6, then 4.6, then 3.4 seconds). Open, its gate is gone, and the
  keepers of the sluices stand down for good.
- From the garden, under the arches: two thornbacks and a lasher.

### The relic: the Carrier's Yoke

*Before the channel was cut, the water came up from the pool on men's shoulders. One of them left this at the head.*
**His flask holds one draught more.**

### What is said

- At the first wheel he turns, once: *A sluice wheel. Its water stops. Struck again, it runs.*
- The first time a keeper undoes him in his hearing: *the keeper has put the wheel back as the house left it.*
- At the head, once: *The head. It comes up from the pool at the bottom of the world. The same water.*
- When the Lock opens: *The Lock is open. The water goes where it was going. Its keepers stand down.*

### As built (build 68), and what was checked

- **294 automated checks pass**, eleven of them the wing's own: its screens, and every wheel on dry stone with
  water of its own; the channel's speed with it, against it and standing; the leap from span to span; the climb and
  the spill barred while their water runs, and all of it reached when it does not; the wheel; the keeper putting a
  wheel back, and standing down; the keeper's blow and the guard against it; the strider on running and on stopped
  water; the Lock's warning, flood, sweep, piers and thirds; the relic; the save's move.
- **Played through by the test browser:** west out of the wellspring against the water; the channel carrying him as
  he stood; the first wheel stopping it and starting it; the ride east over all three gaps and out of the door; a
  jump into the first fall thrown back down; that fall stopped and climbed; a keeper walking to the struck wheel
  and putting it back in three seconds; a keeper striking him; a keeper broken; the Lock's warning and flood, with
  him swept in the water and untouched on a pier; the Lock struck open, its gate gone, through into the head; the
  relic taken and the flask one draught larger; the spill refusing him from below while it ran, and bearing him
  down from the top; its wheel shut.
- **Found and fixed while building:**
  - The first wheel stood in its own water, so he was carried past it before he could strike it. Every wheel is now
    on dry stone, and a check holds that.
  - A running fall could still be climbed by grabbing the ledges in it. There is now no ledge-grab in falling water.
  - Off the end of a span he lost the water's speed at once, so the leap was an ordinary jump. He now keeps it for a
    moment in the air.
  - A blow moved the Lock: struck a few times it slid into its own gate, out of reach. It is fixed in the wall.
  - A strider waited on the span by the door and stung him on his first step in. That span has none now.
  - One who cannot cross the gaps (no glide) had no way back up to the door from the beds. Rungs at the east end.
  - The older checks did not know about falling water, so they walked up the spill and called the head open
    before the Lock. They are taught: the spill's wheel is behind the Lock, and its water runs until then.
- **Not as the plan had it:**
  - The gaps in the channel are not a gate. He has the Wyrm's wind by the time he reaches the Gardens, and a glide
    crosses them with or without the water. The ride is a pleasure and a quick way out, not a lock. The falls are
    the wing's real gates.
  - The plan's sluice gates "shut on him". What shuts on him is the water: a keeper starts a fall again while he is
    in it, and it sets him down.
- **Not checked:** nothing here was played by hand. The sounds. Whether three seconds is fair for a keeper to undo a
  wheel, and whether a keeper is too slow or too strong to fight. Whether the Lock's fight is the right length.
- **A save is moved:** the Gardens stand three rooms further east than they did. The first time a save from before
  is loaded, what it had found and broken in the Gardens is moved with them, once.
