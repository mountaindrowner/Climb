**The edge-grab rule I propose** (your own idea, narrowed): today he takes an edge whenever he is in the air, pressing
toward it, and not holding down. Proposed:

- **Going up, or at the top of a jump:** as now. Pressing toward an edge takes it. This is the jump that just falls
  short, which is what the grab is for.
- **Coming down:** he takes an edge only if **up** is held. Falling past a shelf, he falls past it.
- **Down held:** never, as now.

That keeps the helpful grab and removes the accidental one, without a new button to learn. It is a change to how he
moves, so it is yours to say yes to.

### Words (the largest single theme)

- "The wording is too vague... they're assuming that we know stuff, and it's too poetic." "It speaks too archaically
  or too ambiguously." "It's got to speak player language, not in-game language."
- Examples you hit: *"A full measure pays for a power. What's a measure?"* The Tally's End: *"Once each rest, an Echo
  that catches him does not hurt him. What?"* The Source: *"It holds one draught more, for good... why do we get
  that?"* The grip: no idea how to use it.
- "Too much text" on getting the Warden's spade.
- "Not everything has to have flavor text right off the bat."
- "Everything's the same font... it's just a wall of text."
- **What it asks for:** a design language for words. A plain line that says what a thing does in the player's terms,
  and the flavour kept separate and shorter. This is a pass over every gift, relic, hint and banner.
