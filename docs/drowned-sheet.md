# The Drowned Galleries: the Roots' west wing (the sheet)

*Your word, 26 Nov: "Finish the wings." The world plan (`docs/world-blueprint.md`) gave every region two wings, west
and east. Ten of the twelve are built. Two are not: **the Drowned Galleries** (the Roots, west) and **the Shard** (the
Firmament). This sheet is the Drowned Galleries. Nothing in it is built: it waits on your word. The Shard is at the
end of this sheet.*

## What it is

The first people on the mountain did not climb it. They kept the water at its foot, and lived beside it in halls cut
in the rock. The water rose and took the halls. The pool he wakes in is the last room of them still dry.

**Nine screens, west of the pool. Open from the first:** he can walk into it in his first minute, with nothing but
the sword. It is the Roots' "far left" (decision 0.19).

## Its rule: the water breathes

The halls fill and empty, slowly, the same every time: about twelve seconds rising, a pause, twelve falling, a pause.
Between low water and high water is twelve tiles.

- **At low water** the floors are dry. He walks them. Doors under their lintels are open. What lives in the water lies
  in the last pools.
- **At high water** it bears him up, as all deep water does (phase 16): he floats to ledges, shelves and doorways he
  could not reach from the floor. The doors below are under it.
- **He cannot drown,** and without the Wyrm's wind he cannot go under. So on the first visit the wing is played on the
  water's top and on its floor by turns. With the wind, later, he can go under at high water, and that is where the
  second half of it is.
- **A mark on every wall** shows where high water comes to, so he can see what will be reachable before it rises.

This is the first deep water in the game for one who goes west first, so it teaches floating before the Deep needs it.

*The engine has this already: the Flooded Levels' pump moves its water the same way (`RULE.tide`). What is new is a
clock driving it, and the checks playing each room at low water and at high.*

## Its gates, and the windlasses that work them

*Added on your word, 26 Nov: "Each wing should feel interesting, contextual, complex. Also we should probably have
gates and lever kind of functions that require the players to explore." (Roadmap, decisions 0.32 and 0.33.)*

What the keepers did was hold water. They did it with gates of stone that slide in grooves, each raised and lowered by
a windlass somewhere else, a chain along the wall between the two.

- **A gate holds what is behind it.** Shut at high water, the room behind it stays full while the rest empties: he can
  float in it whenever he likes. Shut at low water, it stays dry while the rest fills. So a gate is not only open or
  shut: it is a choice about a room, made at a moment.
- **A windlass,** struck, turns its gate over (open to shut, shut to open). It stays as he left it, and his save
  remembers it, as it does the Aqueducts' wheels.
- **No windlass is beside its gate.** The chain on the wall says which way it lies. Follow the chain.
- **Some windlasses can only be reached at one state of the water,** or only with another gate set a certain way. That
  is what there is to work out.

**The three gates:**

1. **The hall's gate,** between the water stair and the drowned hall. Shut when he first comes. Its chain runs back up
   the stair to a windlass on a ledge over its head, which only high water reaches. The first lesson, with nothing to
   fight: look where the chain goes, and wait for the water.
2. **The cells' gate,** at the foot of the cells. Its windlass is on the hall's gallery, across the hall. Shut at high
   water, the cells' shaft stays full and he floats straight to the top cell. Shut at low water, it stays dry and he
   can walk into the bottom cell, which is otherwise always under. Both are worth doing: the top cell has the third
   windlass's chain running through it, the bottom cell has a chest.
3. **The culvert's grate,** in the hall's floor. Its windlass is in the cup room, behind the weir: the last room. So
   the quick way back into the pool is opened from the far side, by one who has been all the way through.

**And one he cannot work yet:** a hatch in the hall's floor with no chain to it at all, only a ring. With the Wyrm's
wind he goes under at high water and lifts it from below: the undercroft.

*What is new in the engine for this: a body of water that a gate can hold at the height it had when the gate shut;
the windlass (a struck thing that keeps its state, as the wheels do); the chain drawn along the wall.*

## How it is entered, and left

- **In:** past the pool, a door in the pool room's west wall, and a stair that goes down into the water.
- **Out:** the same way. And, from the far end, **the culvert** (below): the path no one expects.

## The rooms

1. **The water stair** (one screen). A broad stair down from the pool room. At high water its lower half is under. He
   learns the rule here with nothing to fight: stand on the stair and the water comes up to him and lifts him.
2. **The drowned hall** (four screens, two by two: the vast space). The keepers' great hall. A row of columns, a
   gallery round its walls half way up, a floor of small stones set in a picture: people carrying water up a mountain
   on their shoulders. At low water he crosses the floor and sees the picture. At high water he floats to the gallery.
   The way on is off the gallery, so he has to wait for the water. A thread on a column's head, reached only at the
   very top of the rise.
3. **The cells** (two screens, one over the other: the winding way). The keepers' rooms, small, stacked three high,
   each with a low door. Which he can get into depends on the water: he works up through them a rise at a time,
   stepping into a cell before the water falls away under him. Leeches on the wet walls.
4. **The weir** (one screen). Where the keepers held the water back. The wing's fight (below).
5. **The cup room** (one screen). Behind the weir. Dry always. The wing's relic, and a line on the wall in the
   keepers' hand.
6. **The culvert.** In the drowned hall's floor, a drain, open only at low water, too low to stand in: he goes
   through it crouched, east, under the water stair, and comes up **inside the pool he woke in.** The way back, and
   the reason the pool is where it is.

**With the Wyrm's wind, later:** under the hall's floor is the undercroft, always under water: the keepers' store, a
second thread, and what they kept there. Lit faintly from below, as the Flooded Levels are.

## What is in it

By your rules: nothing human, everything drawn flat from the side, and the Roots' missing fourth kind.

- **The skimmer** (new). A long-bodied fly that hangs over water and spits a bead of it in a flat line. Two blows.
  **This is the Roots' flier that shoots,** which the region does not have (`docs/creatures-sheet.md`). It keeps over
  water, so at low water it is confined to the last pools and he can walk round it.
- **The leech** (drawn already, never used). On wet walls and under ledges. It drops on what passes below.
- **The lamprey** (in the game already). On the roofs, as in the Workings.
- **The Pike** (the wing's fight; new). A great blind pike that has grown too big for the weir. At high water the weir
  is its room: it comes at whatever is in the water, and he cannot fight it there. As the water falls it is left in
  the shallows, and then it can be struck. So the fight is the tide: stay out of the water while it is high, on the
  weir's two ledges, and come down as it falls. A fish is seen from the side by nature.

## Its relic

**The Keepers' Cup.** One more drink at each spring. (You made springs one a region in build 88: this gives one back,
for going west first.) Say if you would rather it did something else.

## What it costs

- **Drawings:** the skimmer, the Pike, the floor's picture, a wet variant of the Roots' stone. The skimmer and the
  Pike need the same PixelLab route the creatures pass needs (the picture tool, not the character tool). I have not
  yet shown that route works: the bat I tried came out standing.
- **Builds:** about four. The rooms and the water's clock; the gates and windlasses; the skimmer and the leech; the
  Pike.
- **Risk:** the checks have never played a level whose water moves on a clock. That is the part most likely to take
  longer than I think.

## To decide

1. **Is this what "finish the wings" meant?** I read it as: build the two wings the plan has and the game does not.
2. **The water that breathes, and the gates that hold it: yes?** They are the whole wing. If you would rather the
   water stood still, the wing is simpler and less its own.
3. **The Pike now, or later?** You said of the keepers "only the Wyrm for now". The Pike is a wing's fight, not a
   keeper, but it is a new large creature. The wing can be built without it and the weir left empty.
4. **The relic.**

## The other one: the Shard

The plan's twelfth wing is the Shard: the sliver that broke from the summit the night he fell, reached last, with
everything he has learned. It is phase 18, with the ending, and you asked to be reminded of the story talk before
that is written. So it is not a wing I should design alone: it wants that talk first.
