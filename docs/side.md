# The side view (branch `claude/climb-2d`)

The Climb as a 2.5D side-scroller: a flat plane that climbs up through shafts, tunnels and chambers, metroidvania-style, with HD pixel art from PixelLab and a bright, glowing look. Open `side.html`.

- **World** (`src/side/level.js`): tiles half a unit square (32 pixels), rooms a screen (40 x 23 tiles) or several, joined into a region by doorways, shafts and tunnels. Rooms are built from pieces (walls, doors, ledges, stairs, low tunnels, spikes) and markers (spawn, torches, crystals, creatures, the shrine, the boss, the exit). The first region is twelve rooms on a 4 x 6 grid: up from the start through the corridor, shaft and tunnel to the chamber; then either right along the ascent and gallery to the foot of the well, or up through the roost and across the bridge to the grotto (the shrine) and into the well halfway up; the well climbs two screens to the Warden's arena.
- **Regions**: three, one over the other, each a grid of rooms with its own look (`REGIONS` in `level.js`, `THEMES` in `render.js`): **the Roots** (blue caves; the Warden at the top), **the Cloister** (a ruined monastery: warm brick in even courses, arched windows in the back wall, candles, banners, a bell tower three screens tall, cells to crawl under, two routes round to the chapel) and **the Spires** (the open air near the summit: pale rock in tilted beds, snow on everything that faces up, icicles, prayer flags, falling snow, the sky behind; a climb between a needle and a tower, a broken rope bridge, the peak). One region is in play (`LEVEL`); `loadLevel(n)` refills it and `R.load()` repaints the world, so the way out of one leads into the next without a reload (`enter` in `main.js`). The Spires are not filled in: `fill: EMPTY`, rooms marked `open` draw only their rock. `?level=2` starts in a region; the buttons under the room's name go to any. Both tests run on every region.
- **Creatures** (`src/side/enemies.js`): each has a tell (a red ! over it) and PixelLab strips. `DEF` gives each its numbers, its way of fighting (`ai`) and its drawings. The Roots: crawler, flitter, spitter, burrower. The Cloister: acolyte (ai crawler: a mace, slow, tough), gargoyle (ai flitter: perched like stone until near), wisp (ai spitter: hovers, two bolts). The Spires: yeti (ai thrower: keeps its distance and lobs ice in an arc, swats up close), harpy (ai flitter: two dives), wraith (ai wraith: drifts at him through anything, rears, rushes, hangs spent). A new creature is a `DEF` entry and a marker of its name in a room.
- **The Warden** (`src/side/boss.js`): a stone titan at the back of a two-screen arena, its waist going down into the dark behind the stage. It is a puppet of flat pieces assembled and moved in code. Its arms are three PixelLab pieces (upper arm, forearm, fist) between shoulder, elbow and fist, and the rest is made to that look by `tools/warden-parts.py`: chest with a crystal core, neck and two waist blocks painted by rule (courses of big stone blocks, lit from above, moss on top, runes in the seams; PixelLab will not draw a slab flat on), and a helmed head (cut at the jaw so it opens), a boulder per shoulder and back crystals from PixelLab brought to scale. It breathes, its head follows him, a shoulder rises with its arm and drops with the blow, it reels when a fist is cut, roars (jaw open) as it wakes and at half health, and comes apart piece by piece when it dies. `PIECES` in `boss.js` is the layout; `python3 tools/warden-parts.py sheet out.png` shows the pieces put together.
- **The Toll** (`src/side/toll.js`, the Cloister's chapel) and **the Rime Wyrm** (`src/side/wyrm.js`, the Spires' summit): the other two regions' bosses, puppets like the Warden, their pieces painted by rule (`tools/boss-parts.py`; `sheet out.png` shows them). The Toll is a bell on a chain from a beam that runs along the roof: it swings through where he stood (be to one side or roll under), follows him and slams (then lies on the stage: cut it), and from half health tolls cinders down and its slam ripples. The Wyrm is a head that steers after a point with rings trailing along its path: it sweeps the summit low (jump its head), drives its head into the ground (then it is caught: cut it), and from half health the blow throws up spikes of ice and it breathes a walking line of icicles. A boss is `{ name, hint, rage, fell, step, strike, slam, hud, reset, active, st, room, stageY, cx }`; `main.js` picks one per region.
- **The story** (`src/side/story.js`): the title, the intro (the fall), the card between regions and the ending, drawn 320 x 180 in the palette and scaled up, words over them. `main.js` stops the world while one is up. The mountain picture shows the three regions (crystals, lit windows, snow) and a mark where he is; `REGION_SPAN` is each region's share of the climb, also used by the height gauge on the HUD. Progress (the region reached) is kept in localStorage; `?level=2` or `?play` skips the title.
- **Light** (`render.js`, the last pass): `THEMES[...].amb` is the region's ambient (and `ambIn` inside, where a region has an inside); each light adds a pool, in steps, dithered on the game's pixel grid. Lights are the level's (torches, crystals, shrines), his own, and `R.glint(x, y, colour, power, range, life)` for a moment or a frame (bosses call it for their eyes; blows and kills for a flash). `mist` and `fore` in a theme are the drifting mist and the near shapes; both ride with the camera and slide their pictures.
- **Shape** (normal maps): a strip with `"n": 1` in its sprite.json has `<anim>_n.png` beside it (`python3 tools/pixellab.py normals <folder> [anim ...]`, which also sets the flag; `pull` keeps it). Sprites and boss pieces use `lit()` (`render.js`): a Phong material with the normal map, its light held to `SHADE_STEPS`. The rock's normals are worked out in `paintPlay` from the distance to its edge and from its block seams. `AMB_IN_MAT`, `LIGHT_GAIN` and `LIGHT_Z` balance light from everywhere against light from the torches (low and strong, so it rakes across). Each boss has a light of its own. `?flat` drops every normal map.
- **The combo's effects** (`assets/side/vfx/cut1..3`): made with PixelLab's VFX Creator around the swing's own strip (sent at 14 fps, so effect frame i is strip frame i), with its game pack. `python3 tools/vfx-pack.py <name> <strip>` writes `fx.json`: each layer's sheet and where its top-left is from the drawing's (cx, foot). `makeSwingFx` draws the behind layer under him and the front over, flipped with him; the old drawn crescent is kept for the rising and dash cuts.
- **The scarf** (`src/side/scarf.js`) and **embers**: the scarf is twenty points, each kept a link's length from the last, falling, dragged, blown by the region's wind; `SCARF` says how many links a heart is. `main.js` anchors it at his neck (lower crouched or rolling), burns the end on a hurt, and on a death sends it (`FLIGHT`) to the checkpoint with the camera, then stands him up there. Kills throw embers (`fx.ember`) that home to him; `EMBER.max` (ten) make a heart, and a full ten wait until one is needed.
- **The HUD** (`src/side/hud.js`, `src/side/pixelfont.js`): everything over the game is drawn into a canvas the size of the game's picture and scaled up with it. The face is 5 x 7 capitals (`text`) with a 3 x 5 small print on a dark plate (`fine`). The story's screens use the same face.
- **The score** (`src/side/music.js`): instruments built from oscillators and noise, a sixteenth-note sequencer, a generated reverb. `SONGS` sets the one tune (`MINOR.tune` over `MINOR.prog`) for each place: key (`shift`), tempo, which instruments play what. `main.js` picks the piece each frame (`score`). It cannot be heard in the test machine: it is checked by rendering each piece offline and measuring its level and which notes carry it.
- **Shrine**: fills the flask and the hearts, and is where he wakes after a death.
- **Map**: the region a pixel a tile in the corner, each room drawn once he has been in it, with the shrine and the Warden marked once found.
- **Motor** (`src/side/motor.js`): Movement v2 on tile collision, for a taller warrior (2.4 units, lean gothic-action proportions; `SCALE`: jumps x1.48, run x1.38, gravity x1.22, so he is quick and springy for his height and an arc takes less time, the ledge grab reaching 2.6). Same tuning (`TUNE.move`), the same F9 feel panel and input queue as the climb: the double jump, launch float, air-control fade, coyote time, jump buffer, roll cancels, plunge, heavy landings, the ledge grab; and walls, ceilings, one-way ledges (down + jump drops through), steps climbed as you walk, low tunnels (crouch or roll), spikes.
- **The ledge climb**: the motor carries him up (`P.mantle`, 0.18 to 0.45 s by how far under the ledge he caught it; `wall` is the face he climbs). The drawing (`climb_px`) has no ledge of its own, so `CLIMB` in `main.js` gives, for each frame, the column and row of its canvas that sit on the ledge's corner, and how far his feet hang under it; he enters at the frame that fits. To tune it, set the frames against a drawn corner offline, as the table was made, before trying it in play. `SINK` (two pixels) is how far he is drawn into the floor's lip. The climb drawn now is `climb_hang2`, made from a start frame of him hanging (PixelLab's custom start frame, on his canvas widened to 64); a written starting pose was tried and ignored. The plunge is `plunge_fall` (two frames turning the blade down, then six held) and its landing `plunge_land` (its last eight frames, over half a second, cut short if he moves off).
- **Look** (`src/side/render.js`): stylized flat 2D pixel art on one palette (`assets/side/palette.json`, Endesga 32). The scene renders into a target 180 pixels tall at 16 pixels a unit (the warrior ~28 pixels), orthographic, scaled up to the screen whole; depth is parallax only. The level is painted at load by rules in the palette's colours, not textures: the rock as a base shape (tile corners cut, edges worn except where he stands), a band of chunky stones with a lit lip and moss, dithered steps into a dark silhouette; planks, teeth; decoration drawn in code and fitted to each surface (stalactites, vines, roots, chains, banners, lanterns from ceilings; crystals, mushrooms, bones, rubble, stalagmites on floors), sparse where he walks. Behind: a cracked-stone back wall in parallax with ruined pillars and arches, opening onto the far painting (held to the palette). Torches, crystals, lanterns and the shrine glow; bloom.
- **Organic pass** (`organic()` in `level.js`): rooms are written as boxes, then worn into caves: ceilings sag in smooth noise, walls swell, the top corners take wedges, a cellular step rounds what was added, ramps fill where floors meet walls. It keeps clear the floor he walks and head room over it (more over ledges, where he jumps), doorways and markers. `tests/side-level.test.mjs` checks the result can be played through (a quick model of his reach), and `tests/side-physics.test.mjs` plays it with the real motor in node (`tests/helpers/browser.mjs` stands in for the browser): from every place he can stand it runs a player's inputs (walk, jump, double jumps at several moments, straight up then steer, crouch-walk, drop through) and records where he comes to rest; every room, the shrine, the stage and the exit must be reached, and nowhere he can reach may be a place he cannot get back out of.
- **Art** (`tools/pixellab.py`): characters drawn at 32 pixels (`low detail`), animated with PixelLab's skeleton templates where one fits (idle, run, jump, hurt: the drawing is posed and moved, so it stays itself) and from text otherwise (the cuts, roll, fall, plunge), every frame held to the palette, and snapped to it again on pull. `ppu` in each `sprite.json` says its scale (16 now; older drawings say 32 or 64). Hero options `px_knight`, `px_hood`, `px_mask`, `px_hat` (`?hero=a..d`); creatures `px_crawler`, `px_flitter`, `px_spitter`, `px_burrower`. Characters, side-view animations, backgrounds and tilesets from PixelLab, pulled through the API's own exports and packed into strips (`assets/side/<name>/<anim>.png` + `sprite.json` with where the feet and middle sit). The token is the session's, added by its network proxy; nothing secret is in the repository. `assets/side/pixellab.json` records what was made.

Why PixelLab over rendering our 3D models to sprites (Dead Cells' own pipeline): a bake-off of the same warrior both ways; PixelLab's was crisp hand-pixel art, the render soft and muddy at the same size.

## Animating with weight: the recipe

What made the attacks that read as big and animated (and what made the stiff ones stiff), found by comparing ten takes
of four swings side by side (`tools/anim-sheet.py` makes the sheet):

- **Describe the body, not the weapon.** The good ones say what the legs, hips, torso, shoulders and head do: "he
  crouches", "hops forward", "springs up onto his toes, his whole body stretching", "turns his whole body around",
  "bending deep into a wide forward lunge". The stiff ones described the blade ("a fast forward slash, the blade
  swinging out in front") and the generator moved only the arm.
- **Three beats, named:** a wind-up (a hop, a crouch, a plant, drawing back), the one big motion, and a pose it ends in
  (a deep lunge, a low stance, stretched up on the toes). Say which way the motion goes: "ending low" made a lunge cut
  downward instead of forward; "thrust straight out level in front at chest height" says forward.
- **Enough frames:** 10 to 13. Six or seven leaves no room for a wind-up or a follow-through.
- **Mode:** `pixminimax` first (it also draws the swoosh arcs on the fastest frames), `v3` second. `skeleton-v3`
  keeps the drawing steadiest but moves it stiffly: fine for walking and running, not for swings.
- **In the game:** each swing names its drawing and its impact frame (`BLADE` in `src/side/motor.js`); the wind-up
  plays up to that frame, the cut lands on it, the follow-through plays out the rest.
