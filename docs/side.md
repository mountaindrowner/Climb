# The side view (branch `claude/climb-2d`)

The Climb as a 2.5D side-scroller: a flat plane that climbs up through shafts, tunnels and chambers, metroidvania-style, with HD pixel art from PixelLab and a bright, glowing look. Open `side.html`.

**Direction: a Metroidvania, not a roguelike.** One fixed mountain, learned and gone back through: what he finds, opens and
learns is kept, a fall rewinds him to the water or the last flame, and the world grows by sections that the gifts open
(`docs/world.md`). Nothing is drawn at random per run.

- **World** (`src/side/level.js`): tiles half a unit square (32 pixels), rooms a screen (40 x 23 tiles) or several, joined into a region by doorways, shafts and tunnels. Rooms are built from pieces (walls, doors, ledges, stairs, low tunnels, spikes) and markers (spawn, torches, crystals, creatures, the shrine, the boss, the exit). The first region is twelve rooms on a 4 x 6 grid: up from the start through the corridor, shaft and tunnel to the chamber; then either right along the ascent and gallery to the foot of the well, or up through the roost and across the bridge to the grotto (the shrine) and into the well halfway up; the well climbs two screens to the Warden's arena.
- **Regions**: six, one over the other, each a grid of rooms with its own look. The first three are in `level.js` (`REGIONS`; looks in `paint.js` `THEMES`); the next three are each a file under `src/side/regions/` (see *A region in a file*): **the Roots** (blue caves; the Warden at the top), **the Cloister** (a ruined monastery: warm brick in even courses, arched windows in the back wall, candles, banners, a bell tower three screens tall, cells to crawl under, two routes round to the chapel) and **the Spires** (the open air near the summit: pale rock in tilted beds, snow on everything that faces up, icicles, prayer flags, falling snow, the sky behind; a climb between a needle and a tower, a broken rope bridge, the peak). One region is in play (`LEVEL`); `loadLevel(n)` refills it and `R.load()` repaints the world, so the way out of one leads into the next without a reload (`enter` in `main.js`). The Spires are not filled in: `fill: EMPTY`, rooms marked `open` draw only their rock. `?level=2` starts in a region; the buttons under the room's name go to any. Both tests run on every region.
- **Creatures** (`src/side/enemies.js`): each has a tell (a red ! over it) and PixelLab strips. `DEF` gives each its numbers, its way of fighting (`ai`) and its drawings. The Roots: crawler, flitter, spitter, burrower. The Cloister: acolyte (ai crawler: a mace, slow, tough), gargoyle (ai flitter: perched like stone until near), wisp (ai spitter: hovers, two bolts). The Spires: yeti (ai thrower: keeps its distance and lobs ice in an arc, swats up close), harpy (ai flitter: two dives), wraith (ai wraith: drifts at him through anything, rears, rushes, hangs spent). A new creature is a `DEF` entry and a marker of its name in a room.
- **The Warden** (`src/side/boss.js`): a stone titan at the back of a two-screen arena, its waist going down into the dark behind the stage. It is a puppet of flat pieces assembled and moved in code. Its arms are three PixelLab pieces (upper arm, forearm, fist) between shoulder, elbow and fist, and the rest is made to that look by `tools/warden-parts.py`: chest with a crystal core, neck and two waist blocks painted by rule (courses of big stone blocks, lit from above, moss on top, runes in the seams; PixelLab will not draw a slab flat on), and a helmed head (cut at the jaw so it opens), a boulder per shoulder and back crystals from PixelLab brought to scale. It breathes, its head follows him, a shoulder rises with its arm and drops with the blow, it reels when a fist is cut, roars (jaw open) as it wakes and at half health, and comes apart piece by piece when it dies. `PIECES` in `boss.js` is the layout; `python3 tools/warden-parts.py sheet out.png` shows the pieces put together.
- **The Toll** (`src/side/toll.js`, the Cloister's chapel) and **the Rime Wyrm** (`src/side/wyrm.js`, the Spires' summit): the other two regions' bosses, puppets like the Warden, their pieces painted by rule (`tools/boss-parts.py`; `sheet out.png` shows them). The Toll is a bell on a chain from a beam that runs along the roof: it swings through where he stood (be to one side or roll under), follows him and slams (then lies on the stage: cut it), and from half health tolls cinders down and its slam ripples. The Wyrm is a head that steers after a point with rings trailing along its path: it sweeps the summit low (jump its head), drives its head into the ground (then it is caught: cut it), and from half health the blow throws up spikes of ice and it breathes a walking line of icicles. A boss is `{ name, hint, rage, fell, step, strike, slam, hud, reset, active, st, room, stageY, cx }`; `main.js` picks one per region.
- **A region in a file** (`src/side/regions/<key>.js`, the Tempest, the Gardens, the Firmament): its default export is the whole region: `name`, `theme` (its key), `sub` / `card` / `epithet` (the words under its name, on its card, under its keeper's), `quiet` (the keeper's stage, left flat by the organic pass), `seed`, `wear`, `fill` / `sky`, `layout` (rooms on a grid, built from `rooms.js`), and optionally `themeDef` (a `THEMES` entry), `deco(px, PAL)` (new decoration), `enemies` (entries for `DEF`), `ai` (ways of fighting: `ai[name](e, P, dt, ctx)`), `songs` (the generated score's pieces) and `tick(dt, t, ctx)` (weather). `level.js`, `render.js`, `enemies.js`, `music.js` and `main.js` read these (`REGION_DATA`); the file is also loaded by the tests in node, so it imports nothing but `rooms.js`. Its keeper is a module named in `src/side/keepers.js`. A seventh region is a file, a keeper, and a line in `REGIONS` and `KEEPERS`.
- **Painting, off the page** (`paint.js`, `paintworker.js`, `render.js` `painted()`): the painter (the play rock with its normal map, the back wall, what the marks draw into it) is its own module with no page and no three.js in it, so a worker runs it: `painted(n)` asks the worker for region n's paintings and keeps them (at most three; a region's painting never changes, since cracked rock and star-glass are drawn apart), and the region above him is asked for while he climbs this one, so going up never waits on it. Where the page allows no worker (or with `?noworker`), the region he is in is painted on the page, as before. The paintings are cut into pieces of 1024 pixels and sent to the graphics card one at a time before the region is shown (`UPLOAD`), and its shaders compiled then; the fade in waits for it (`R.isReady`). Canvases read back (`getImageData`) are made `willReadFrequently`, which keeps them off the graphics card.
- **The Deep, off the climb** (`regions/workings.js`; `deep: true`): a region that is not one of the climb's, under the Roots. `CLIMB_TOP` (`level.js`) is the summit's index: the way on, the ending, the summit's music and the next region's sky in the arch go by the climb, not by the list, and reaching the Deep does not raise `G.top`. `depth` is its place below the water on the height bar (negative metres); `musicOf` and `ambOf` give it another region's music and air; `looks` dresses its first room as where he came from.
- **Passages** (`pass` and `arrive` marks; `main.js` `PASS`, `go(n, at)`, `enter(n, at)`): a `pass` (x, y, w, h, `to`, `at`) is a zone that takes him to region `to`, where he comes in at the `arrive` mark named `at`. The Roots' Pithead (off the corridor) drops down the old cage shaft to the Workings' cage; the top of that shaft climbs back out to the Pithead. Not again until he has been out of one a moment. The renderer paints a passage's far side ahead of him (`want`).
- **Played with the gifts he has** (`tests/helpers/reach.mjs`; `tests/side-physics.test.mjs`; `tools/gifts-report.mjs`): the real-motor graph of a region is built for a set of gifts (`graph(N, gifts)`: the Warden's weight breaks the cracked stone, the Toll's step adds air-dash moves, the Wyrm's wind adds glides where there is rising air). Each region is tested with what he has when he comes (`arrival`: the keepers below it; the Deep, nothing) and with every gift: on arrival every room not behind a gate, the shrine, the stage and the way out are reached (so no region needs a gift given above it, and no keeper its own); with every gift, every room; a gate's rooms stay shut without its gift; no traps either way; and the nearest flame is within 40 s at a run of where the region leads. `node tools/gifts-report.mjs` writes `docs/gifts-report.md`: what each later gift newly opens, room by room, and every flame's runback.
- **Embers and the ember arts** (`main.js` `EMBER`, `art`, `stepArts`, `waves`; `motor.js` `ARTS`, `startArt`; `enemies.js` `hitBox`; the `art` action, E / middle mouse / I, RB on a pad, a touch button): embers come from fallen creatures (they fly to him) and from every blow of the blade that lands (one each), up to three measures of ten (`EMBER.cap`). They no longer mend him by themselves: a measure mends a heart when he presses the flask key with the flask dry (seven with the Gardener's Seed), or pays for an ember art. The art key with down is **the slam** (the Warden's weight): a stamp, or from the air a plunge, and the blow runs along the floor both ways (`waves`), striking what it reaches, breaking cracked stone, stopping at a wall or the floor's end. On the ground it is **the cleave** (the Toll's step): a level rush through everything in the way, untouchable. In the air it is **the gust** (the Wyrm's wind): up, turning, throwing what is round him. Each needs its gift (`ART_GIFT`); an art's blows (`hitBox`, once a creature) give no embers back. The HUD shows the measures under the hearts, a full one glinting. Each gift's message says how to use its art.
- **Blade and spade** (`motor.js` `SPADE`, `weaponOf`; `main.js` the `swap` and `kill` events, `armed`, `swapButton`; the `swap` action, R / U, LB on a pad, a touch button shown once he has it): the Sexton drops his spade when he falls, once (`G.arms`), with a card. The swap key changes what he holds (`G.wield`, kept). The spade is one great swing (`atk_spade`, drawn by PixelLab, about 3 cents with `spade_idle`): slow to lift, 3 damage, a longer reach than the blade, and it breaks cracked stone where it lands, every swing (`digs`); no combo, no rising or dashing cut, and no guard (`guard: false`: block does nothing). The blade keeps its combo, its rising and dashing cuts, and the guard and parry. Standing, he leans on the spade (`spade_idle`). `tests/side-arms.test.mjs` checks every swing names a drawing he has, lit, and the spade's shape. `sprite.json` is fetched with the build in its address, so a new drawing is never met with an old list.
- **Nowhere to be stuck** (`motor.js` `shove`, `unstick`, `tryPlank`; `tests/helpers/stuck.mjs`; `tests/side-stuck.test.mjs`): a creature moving him sideways goes through `shove`, which stops at rock; each step, if his body (crouched) is in rock he is moved to the nearest open place. The stuck-finder plays every place he could stand that open air joins to where he plays (in rooms) with the reachability graph's input programs, up to six moves on; any place from which he cannot get back is a pocket, and the test fails. Pockets the wear leaves are filled in `level.js` `FILL`. He catches a plank overhead anywhere along it (`tryPlank`); after a drop through a plank (`P.dropping`, until he lands) or while holding down, he catches no edge.
- **Placing what clings and perches** (`level.js` `clingers`, `CLINGS`, the gargoyle perches; `tests/side-place.test.mjs`): spitters are put against a wall within twelve tiles (or on a nearby row that has one), lampreys under a roof within seventeen (or a wall); gargoyles on a corbel out of a wall (searched a few rows up and down, then further across), else a plinth on rock three wide, never within eight tiles of one of the people.
- **Scarf knots** (`state.js` `KNOTS`, `knots()`, `wear()`, `has()`, `owns()`; `main.js` `knotMenu`, `drawKnots`; `scarf.js` the knots drawn): a relic works only while it is tied into his scarf. `G.relics` is what he carries, `G.worn` what he wears; `has(id)` asks whether it is worn (every relic's effect goes through it), `owns(id)` whether it is carried. Two knots to start; Ordric ties more for shards (`KNOTS.cost`), to five (`KNOTS.most`), against eight relics, so there is always a choice. A relic found is tied at once if a knot is free; otherwise he carries it. At a flame, down (with any relic carried) opens the knots: pick a relic to tie or untie it; with every knot tied it is refused. Each worn relic is a small gold knot along the scarf. A save from before the knots wears what it carried and is given a knot for each, up to five (`knotted`). `tests/side-knots.test.mjs`.
- **Wings and gates** (`regions/ossuary.js`; `gate` on a room): a wing is rooms laid into a region beside its climb (the Ossuary: `OSSUARY.LAYOUT` in the Roots' layout, with its `LOOKS` and `QUIET`), closed by something he learns. A room's `gate` (`'breaker'`: cracked stone) is carried to `LEVEL.rooms`, and the tests play the region both ways: `side-level` and `side-physics` require a gated room to be shut with the cracked stone whole and open with it broken, and look for traps both ways; `side-items` asks for one relic on the climb and one in each wing. A shortcut out of a wing is a drop from a ledge too high to be reached from below (the motor's ledge grab reaches about 8 tiles: the test catches it if not). A creature with `scale` is drawn that much larger, its feet kept on the floor; `dmg` is how many hearts its blow takes.
- **A room's look fades only toward an opening** (`render.js` `opensTo`): toward a side that opens into a room of another look, not toward a floor, a wall, the world's edge or more of the same look.
- **A keeper's entrance** (`src/side/entrance.js`, `docs/keepers.md`; `main.js` `ENT`): when a keeper wakes, before its own rise: its hall seals (`openings`: the doorways on its sides and the shafts through its floor, shut at their mouths; the tiles become rock), then the dread, then its rise, and at the keeper's moment in the rise (`reveal`, on its `B.tm`) its name big (`hud.js` `title`) with its music cut in on the beat (`score.js` `play(key, hard)`, loaded ahead with `load`) and its burst. Each keeper's own `STYLES` entry: seal (stone, grate, ice, storm, thorn, glass), dread (heart, toll, frost, static, roots, stars), when it is revealed, its burst, its colours. While it leads in (`ENT.lead`) the keeper, the creatures and he are held, the music and ambience are hushed (`ENT.hush`), the picture darkens to the keeper's tint and closes in (`HUD.dark`, `vignette`, the Wyrm's `sweep`), the lights dim (`render.js` `MOOD`). The first time is long; after it (`G.keepers`) short, and jump or attack skips it. Flashes are capped below a white-out and the Thunderhead's lightning at two a second; the Flashes option turns them off. When it falls the seals break (`open`); if he falls they are gone (`reset`).
- **The seams** (`src/side/seams.js` `SEAMS[n]`: `rows`, `link`, `layout`): the short ways between two regions of the climb (the Crypt Stair, the Bell Road, the Last Hut, the Breach, the Long Stair), each built under region n's first room: `level.js` `build` moves the region up `rows` rooms, lays the seam's rooms beneath it (rock beside them, even under a region of sky), keeps only the seam's spawn, and cuts the hole at `link` (tile columns) up into the region, boarded over, so he can drop back down. A seam room has a `title`, which the place line shows. The foot wears the region below's look, the head the region's own. The ways up to a hole are ledges stacked in the hole's own columns (`shaftUp`): from beside it, the hole's rock edge is in the way of a jump. Saves from before the seams are moved up with the region (`state.js` `seamed`). First pass: three screens each, where `docs/world.md` plans six to ten.
- **Phone mode** (`OPT.phone`: auto, on, off; `render.js` `LOW`; Options, 'Phone mode'): on by itself for a touch screen no wider than 1400. It keeps one painted region, not three, paints nothing ahead, and draws half the air's motes and three lights, not more. Not done yet: smaller paintings (the far painting at half size), a lower drawing size, fewer creatures awake, and a check on a real phone. A change takes effect from the next region.
- **The spitters, a family** (`enemies.js`): the Black Leech (the Roots), the Sump Lamprey and the Pit Barnacle (the Workings), the Fountain Toad (the Gardens): the same way of fighting, tougher the further on (the lamprey spits twice, the toad lobs and takes more). `cling` sets one against what it holds to, by its drawing's box (`sprite.json` `box`): a wall (its rock to the wall, facing out, never turning), a ceiling (hanging; with none near, a wall, turned a quarter), or the floor. `variants` picks one of several drawings by where it is. `mouthY` is where a hanging one spits from.
- **The pool** (`props.js`): the water at the bottom of the world in its cistern: a basin cut into the floor of the first room, the water deep and lit, an apse behind it (`apseTex`), threads of water falling into it (`fallTex`). `plain: true` on a pool mark is only water (the Workings' cistern).
- **Creatures asleep** (`enemies.js` `SLEEP`): one at rest (idle, circling, drifting, perched) more than 24 units across or 15 up or down from him does nothing and is not drawn until he comes near; one in the middle of something finishes it.
- **The map** (`hud.js` `map`, `fullmap`; `main.js` `mapDots`, `mapScreen`): the corner map is at one scale (a pixel for two tiles) whatever the size of the region, centred on him and scrolling with him; the whole map is in the pause (and on M, or Select), the region fitted to the picture, his room outlined, with a key.
- **One region into the next** (`look` on a room; `render.js` `blend`, `LOOKW`): `level.js` gives each region's first room the look of the region below (`looks: { room: theme }` or `vestibule: false` in a region overrides). The renderer paints the play layer, its normals and the back wall in both looks and lays the other into that room's rectangle with a ragged, dithered edge; a second far painting, mist, near shapes and motes are made for the other look, and `LOOKW` (how deep in such a room he is, eased from its edge over five units) fades them, the ambient light with them. `R.look()` is the look he is in: `main.js` plays that region's piece and ambience, blows the scarf by it, and holds the region's name until he walks out. The way on (the `exit` marker) is an arch with a strip of the next region's far painting in it; its air drifts out (`main.js`) and its ambience is heard near it.
- **Tiles and what changes** (`src/side/rooms.js`, `src/side/props.js`): beside rock, ledges and spikes there is cracked rock (4: rock until broken) and star-glass (5, 6: a ledge for one half of `PHASE.period` or the other). `tileAt` gives the tile as he meets it now, `rawAt` as drawn; the painted layers leave both out and `props.js` draws them (and blooms, and the streaks in rising air) as meshes of their own. Markers `bloom` and `updraft { w, h }` become `LEVEL.pads` and `LEVEL.updrafts`, which the motor asks.
- **What he learns** (`CAN` in `motor.js`, `TEACH` in `state.js`): `breaker` (the plunge and the finishing cut break cracked rock: `breakRock` in `main.js`), `dash` (a roll begun in the air flies level, `AIRDASH`), `glide` (a third jump press held, or jump held in rising air: `GLIDE`). Set from `G.beat` (which keepers have fallen) by `learn()`; a region reached by its address (`?level=4`) has what the keepers below it teach. The tests play with all three and with star-glass always there.
- **Things to find** (`src/side/items.js`, markers `urn`, `chest { holds }`, `thread`, `npc { who }`): urns and chests break to a cut or a plunge (down opens a chest too) and spill shards, which lie where they fall and come to him when near. A chest holds shards, a thread, or `relic:<id>` (`RELICS` in `state.js`: what each does is a line in `learn()`, `onEvent` or `items.js`). `tests/side-items.test.mjs` checks each region has one relic, two threads and the three people, all standing on something.
- **A fall** (`main.js`: `TRAIL`, `FLIGHT`, `raise`, `refill`; `story.js` `pool`): where he has been since he last rose is kept (a sample every twentieth of a second, the far end thinned). When his hearts are gone it is run back, slowly then faster, with afterimages and the score run fast; then the pool's screen; then `raise` stands him at the last flame (or the region's start), makes the creatures again (`refill`) and resets the keeper. `G.falls` counts. The pool in the Roots' first room is the marker `pool { w }`, drawn by `props.js`. What it means: `docs/story.md`.
- **People** (`src/side/talk.js`; the markers still call them lamplighter, wren, scribe; their names are Ordric of the Vigil, Mirel the Penitent, Corwen the Annalist): `SCRIPT[who][region]` is what each says there (all of it the first time, its last line after); `makeTalk()` draws the box (a face cut from their standing drawing, the words typed out). The world waits while someone speaks. What Ordric can do for shards is `WARES` in `main.js`.
- **The save and the options** (`src/side/state.js`): `G` is everything kept (region, keepers beaten, shards, threads, hearts, flask, relics, what was taken and broken where, who was met); `OPT` the settings. Both in localStorage (`climb-side`, `climb-side-opts`). `born()` in `main.js` makes him with the hearts and flask he has earned.
- **Sound** (`src/audio/sfx.js`, `src/audio/score.js`, `assets/audio/`): `sfx.<name>()` plays the recording of that name if `manifest.json` lists one (`USE` maps a name to a recording, its level and how far its pitch strays) and the synthesised stand-in if not, so a call never fails and a new recording is used as soon as it is packed. `ambience(name)` loops one under a region. The recorded score is one decoded piece at a time through the music bus, faded between; `main.js` `score()` picks the piece and falls back to the generated score (`music.js`) where there is no recording. To add or redo sound: generate with PixelLab (`/sound-effects`, `/music`; the files are on backblaze.pixellab.ai, which the environment must allow), put the downloads in a folder as `<name>.wav` / `<key>_1.mp3`, `<key>_2.mp3`, and run `python3 tools/audio-pack.py <folder>` (trims, levels, picks the take with least dead air, writes the manifest).
- **A controller** (`src/core/input.js` `pollPads` for play, bound in `SET.pad`; `main.js` `padUI` for everything round it): menus take up/down, A to pick, B to go back (`menuShow`'s `back`), Start pauses; `HINT.pad` names the bound buttons; `buzz` rumbles on events, off with `OPT.rumble`.
- **Touch** (`side.html`, `src/core/input.js`): `#stick.float` is a zone over the left of the screen; a touch in it is the stick's centre (a ring, `#thumb`, comes up there), and the centre is dragged along if the thumb runs past the ring.
- **The story** (`src/side/story.js`): the title, the intro (the fall), the card between regions and the ending, drawn 320 x 180 in the palette and scaled up, words over them. `main.js` stops the world while one is up. The mountain picture shows the six regions (crystals, lit windows, snow and needles, the storm, terraces, the observatory under the beacon) and a mark where he is; `REGION_SPAN` is each region's share of the climb, also used by the height gauge on the HUD. `?level=2` or `?play` skips the title.
- **Light** (`render.js`, the last pass): `THEMES[...].amb` is the region's ambient (and `ambIn` inside, where a region has an inside); each light adds a pool, in steps, dithered on the game's pixel grid. Lights are the level's (torches, crystals, shrines), his own, and `R.glint(x, y, colour, power, range, life)` for a moment or a frame (bosses call it for their eyes; blows and kills for a flash). `mist` and `fore` in a theme are the drifting mist and the near shapes; both ride with the camera and slide their pictures.
- **Shape** (normal maps): a strip with `"n": 1` in its sprite.json has `<anim>_n.png` beside it (`python3 tools/pixellab.py normals <folder> [anim ...]`, which also sets the flag; `pull` keeps it). Sprites and boss pieces use `lit()` (`render.js`): a Phong material with the normal map, its light held to `SHADE_STEPS`. The rock's normals are worked out in `paintPlay` from the distance to its edge and from its block seams. `AMB_IN_MAT`, `LIGHT_GAIN` and `LIGHT_Z` balance light from everywhere against light from the torches (low and strong, so it rakes across). Each boss has a light of its own. `?flat` drops every normal map.
- **The combo's effects** (`assets/side/vfx/cut1..3`): made with PixelLab's VFX Creator around the swing's own strip (sent at 14 fps, so effect frame i is strip frame i), with its game pack. `python3 tools/vfx-pack.py <name> <strip>` writes `fx.json`: each layer's sheet and where its top-left is from the drawing's (cx, foot). `makeSwingFx` draws the behind layer under him and the front over, flipped with him; the old drawn crescent is kept for the rising and dash cuts.
- **The scarf** (`src/side/scarf.js`) and **embers**: the scarf is thirty points, each kept a link's length from the last, falling, dragged, blown by the region's wind; `SCARF` says how many links a heart is. `main.js` anchors it at his neck (lower crouched or rolling), burns the end on a hurt, and on a death sends it (`FLIGHT`) to the checkpoint with the camera, then stands him up there. Kills throw embers (`fx.ember`) that home to him; `EMBER.max` (ten) make a heart, and a full ten wait until one is needed.
- **The HUD** (`src/side/hud.js`, `src/side/pixelfont.js`): everything over the game is drawn into a canvas the size of the game's picture and scaled up with it. The face is 5 x 7 capitals (`text`) with a 3 x 5 small print on a dark plate (`fine`). The story's screens use the same face.
- **The score** (`src/side/music.js`): instruments built from oscillators and noise, a sixteenth-note sequencer, a generated reverb. `SONGS` sets the one tune (`MINOR.tune` over `MINOR.prog`) for each place: key (`shift`), tempo, which instruments play what. `main.js` picks the piece each frame (`score`). It cannot be heard in the test machine: it is checked by rendering each piece offline and measuring its level and which notes carry it.
- **Shrine**: fills the flask and the hearts, and is where he wakes after a death.
- **Map**: the region a pixel a tile in the corner, each room drawn once he has been in it, with the shrine and the Warden marked once found.
- **Motor** (`src/side/motor.js`): Movement v2 on tile collision, for a taller warrior (2.4 units, lean gothic-action proportions; `SCALE`: jumps x1.48, run x1.38, gravity x1.22, so he is quick and springy for his height and an arc takes less time, the ledge grab reaching 2.6). Same tuning (`TUNE.move`), the same F9 feel panel and input queue as the climb: the double jump, launch float, air-control fade, coyote time, jump buffer, roll cancels, plunge, heavy landings, the ledge grab; and walls, ceilings, one-way ledges (down + jump drops through), steps climbed as you walk, low tunnels (crouch or roll), spikes.
- **The ledge climb**: the motor carries him up (`P.mantle`, 0.18 to 0.45 s by how far under the ledge he caught it; `wall` is the face he climbs). The drawing (`climb_px`) has no ledge of its own, so `CLIMB` in `main.js` gives, for each frame, the column and row of its canvas that sit on the ledge's corner, and how far his feet hang under it; he enters at the frame that fits. To tune it, set the frames against a drawn corner offline, as the table was made, before trying it in play. `SINK` (two pixels) is how far he is drawn into the floor's lip. The climb drawn now is `climb_hang2`, made from a start frame of him hanging (PixelLab's custom start frame, on his canvas widened to 64); a written starting pose was tried and ignored. The plunge is `plunge_fall` (two frames turning the blade down, then six held) and its landing `plunge_land` (its last eight frames, over half a second, cut short if he moves off).
- **Look** (`src/side/render.js`): stylized flat 2D pixel art on one palette (`assets/side/palette.json`, Endesga 32). The scene renders into a target 180 pixels tall at 16 pixels a unit (the warrior ~28 pixels), orthographic, scaled up to the screen whole; depth is parallax only. The level is painted at load by rules in the palette's colours, not textures: the rock as a base shape (tile corners cut, edges worn except where he stands), a band of chunky stones with a lit lip and moss, dithered steps into a dark silhouette; planks, teeth; decoration drawn in code and fitted to each surface (stalactites, vines, roots, chains, banners, lanterns from ceilings; crystals, mushrooms, bones, rubble, stalagmites on floors), sparse where he walks. Behind: a cracked-stone back wall in parallax with ruined pillars and arches, opening onto the far painting (held to the palette). Torches, crystals, lanterns and the shrine glow; bloom.
- **Organic pass** (`organic()` in `level.js`): rooms are written as boxes, then worn into caves: ceilings sag in smooth noise, walls swell, the top corners take wedges, a cellular step rounds what was added, ramps fill where floors meet walls. It keeps clear the floor he walks and head room over it (more over ledges, where he jumps), doorways and markers. `tests/side-level.test.mjs` checks the result can be played through (a quick model of his reach), and `tests/side-physics.test.mjs` plays it with the real motor in node (`tests/helpers/browser.mjs` stands in for the browser): from every place he can stand it runs a player's inputs (walk, jump, double jumps at several moments, straight up then steer, crouch-walk, drop through) and records where he comes to rest; every room, the shrine, the stage and the exit must be reached, and nowhere he can reach may be a place he cannot get back out of.
- **Art** (`tools/pixellab.py`): characters drawn at 32 pixels (`low detail`), animated with PixelLab's skeleton templates where one fits (idle, run, jump, hurt: the drawing is posed and moved, so it stays itself) and from text otherwise (the cuts, roll, fall, plunge), every frame held to the palette, and snapped to it again on pull. `ppu` in each `sprite.json` says its scale (16 now; older drawings say 32 or 64). Hero options `px_knight`, `px_hood`, `px_mask`, `px_hat` (`?hero=a..d`); creatures `px_crawler`, `px_flitter`, `px_spitter`, `px_burrower`. Characters, side-view animations, backgrounds and tilesets from PixelLab, pulled through the API's own exports and packed into strips (`assets/side/<name>/<anim>.png` + `sprite.json` with where the feet and middle sit). The token is the session's, added by its network proxy; nothing secret is in the repository. `assets/side/pixellab.json` records what was made.

Why PixelLab over rendering our 3D models to sprites (Dead Cells' own pipeline): a bake-off of the same warrior both ways; PixelLab's was crisp hand-pixel art, the render soft and muddy at the same size.

## Animating with weight: the recipe

What made the attacks that read as big and animated (and what made the stiff ones stiff), found by comparing ten takes
of four swings side by side (`tools/anim-sheet.py` makes the sheet):

- **Describe the body, not the weapon.** The good ones say what the legs, hips, torso, shoulders and head do: "he
  crouches", "hops forward", "springs up onto his toes, his whole body stretching", "turns his whole body around",
  "bending deep into a wide forward lunge". The stiff ones described the blade ("a fast forward slash, the blade
  swinging out in front") and the generator moved only the arm.
- **Three beats, named:** a wind-up (a hop, a crouch, a plant, drawing back), the one big motion, and a pose it ends in
  (a deep lunge, a low stance, stretched up on the toes). Say which way the motion goes: "ending low" made a lunge cut
  downward instead of forward; "thrust straight out level in front at chest height" says forward.
- **Enough frames:** 10 to 13. Six or seven leaves no room for a wind-up or a follow-through.
- **Mode:** `pixminimax` first (it also draws the swoosh arcs on the fastest frames), `v3` second. `skeleton-v3`
  keeps the drawing steadiest but moves it stiffly: fine for walking and running, not for swings.
- **In the game:** each swing names its drawing and its impact frame (`BLADE` in `src/side/motor.js`); the wind-up
  plays up to that frame, the cut lands on it, the follow-through plays out the rest.
