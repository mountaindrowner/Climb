# The Climb: what it is about

Notes for anyone writing a line, naming a thing, or building a region. The game alludes; it does not explain. Nothing
below is ever said outright in the game, and no line in the game should say it for us.

## The shape of it

He stood at the top of the world, beside a flame he was given to keep. He let it die (`What is true`, below, says how;
the game never does), and he fell: the whole height of
the mountain, into the water at its roots. The water gave him back. Far above there is a light, and it is calling him
home. He was not made to stay where he was left.

That is all the game tells. What it is about:

- **He is not climbing to earn anything.** He goes up because that is where he belongs, not because the top is a prize.
  No one is asking a price. (Mirel thinks someone is; see below.)
- **It is not his strength that brings him up.** Every fall ends in the water at the bottom of the world, and the water
  gives him back, every time, at no cost. The red thread at his neck was tied on him; he did not make it. Rising air
  carries him. Flames are kept for him by someone else. He is helped at every turn and deserves none of it.
- **What bars the way is what was left behind.** The keepers are not evil. Each is something that went on doing its duty
  after the flame went dark, and forgot what the duty was for. The last of them is himself: what he was when he let the
  flame die, still standing at the top, sure that it is him.
- **The light was never only at the top.** The ending says so in one line: it had gone down into the dark to find him.

## What is true (never said)

The whole of what happened, written down so that every fragment in the game agrees with it (`docs/writing.md`, rule 3).
None of this is said in the game. Pieces of it are shown.

- **The night of the fall.** He kept the flame in a cradle of stone at the summit. The people below had stopped looking
  up, and he thought the flame needed carrying to them, and that it needed *him* to carry it. He lifted it out of its
  cradle and started down. It went out in his hands on the first ledge. He fell with it, the whole height of the
  mountain. *Shown:* the empty cradle at the summit; the scorch of it on the stone; the Fallen carries a dark lamp and
  will not put it down; Ordric, in the storm: "I always wanted to do the carrying."
- **Everyone below did the same thing he did.** The house, the guild, the Wrights, the astronomers: each tried to make
  the light themselves. That is why the mountain is the way it is. The flame was never theirs, or his, to carry.
- **The water.** It runs under the whole mountain, from the Source at the bottom of the Hollow up to the pool, and up
  again by the Aqueducts to the Gardens. It was there before the house or the guild. It gives back whatever falls into it.
- **The thread.** Tied on him in the water, the first time. By whom is the one thing this file does not say either.
- **The light at the bottom.** When the flame went out at the top, the light did not end. It had gone down: the flame
  beside the Source, which no one keeps, is the same light. The ending's one line is true literally.
- **The shard.** The piece of the summit that broke when he lifted the flame out. It held the light so long that some is
  still in it.
- **The Fallen.** What he was in that moment, sure the flame needed him, left at the top still holding the dark lamp. It
  is not killed: when it is spent, he takes it back. The dark runs off it and its scarf goes to him.

## Voice

Plain, grave, short. Old words where a plain one exists (flame, water, thread, vigil, raised, kept). No exclamation.
No jokes. Things are described by what they do and what they have forgotten, in the manner of an inscription or an
item's note: *It rings for a light it cannot see.* A line may be bleak; the game is not. Never name the allusion,
never quote scripture, never preach. If a line would work on a church poster, cut it.

## The things

| In the game | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The water** (the pool in the first room; the screen after a fall) | Where he woke, and where every fall ends. It gives back what falls into it. His flask is filled with it. |
| **The red thread** (the scarf) | His life, a length for each heart. Tied on him, not his own. It burns down when he is hurt and mends at a flame. Strands of it are caught in high places; three make a heart. Others who climb have a grey one. |
| **A flame** (a shrine) | Kept alight for him by Ordric. Where he is raised after a fall. |
| **A fall** (a death) | Everything he did since he last rose runs back, fast, to the water. The creatures are there again. What he found, lit and learned is kept. The game counts falls as "raised N times". |
| **The flame above** (the beacon) | What he was given to keep. Dark, he thinks. |
| **The scar** (a pale line down the mountain on the title) | The way he fell. In the ending the light comes down it. |
| **The shard** (the sliver hanging beside the summit) | It broke from the top the night he fell and has not come down. Unexplained, and it should stay so. |

## The mountain

Too large to see whole: its foot runs out of the picture on both sides, whole ranges come up to its knees, the moon
stands behind its shoulder, and its peak leans out over its own base. Small things of people are on it for scale and
for sorrow: a castle on a foothill, a village's lights, a watchtower with one window lit, the Cloister's bell tower
standing off its face. People live in sight of it and do not look up.

From the foot: **the Roots** (the dark, and the water), **the Cloister** (a house that kept the hours of the flame, and
kept ringing after), **the Spires** (the mountain's bones, and the pass), **the Tempest** (a storm that was not there
while the flame burned), **the Gardens** (planted for the flame's keepers; no one told the garden they were gone) and
**the Firmament** (the summit, where the stars are close and he has been before). Each region's way in still looks like
the region below, and each way on shows the sky of the one above: it is one mountain, and it should be hard to believe.

## Who is on the mountain

He and the Fallen are the only human shapes alive on it. The humans who were here, the house, the guild, the Wrights, the
astronomers and the pilgrims, are the ones who tried to carry the light themselves; they are gone, and what is left of
them is ruins, statues, and what they became. Those who live there now are the mountain's own folk, creatures who stayed:
an old moth, a hermit crab, a rook, a mole. They speak, they climb, they keep things; they are not human, and he is.
Nothing in the game remarks on it.

## The three who climb

In the level files they are still `lamplighter`, `wren` and `scribe` (marker names); their names and lines are in
`src/side/talk.js`.

- **Ordric of the Vigil.** An old moth. He keeps the flames and has never flown at one. Keeps the flames because he was asked to and no one has told him to stop. Faithful past
  the point of understanding why, and dry about it. He can do a little for shards; "the rest was never mine to do."
- **Mirel the Penitent.** A hermit crab, a pilgrim under a great borrowed shell: everything she thinks she owes, and not
  even hers. She means to arrive deserving the
  flame, and counts her falls so they can be held against her. The water keeps giving her back anyway. In the storm she
  cannot carry the shell; in the Gardens she has put it down and does not know what to do with her hands; at the top she
  waits, unhurried. She is the game's argument, made in six short meetings, and she must never state it.
- **Corwen the Annalist.** A rook. Records the mountain exactly and cannot account for it. He also says what is still unfound
  where he stands. His last page is left open.

## The whole mountain, and what is under it

The mountain at three times its size, from the mines three thousand metres under the water to the shard above the
summit, is planned section by section in `docs/world.md`: each section's history, what is happening there now, who is in
it, its light and its rooms. That file also holds the second thing under the story, never said in the game either: the
mountain is also what anxiety is like from inside (the dark you dig down into, the hours rung for nothing, the storm
always about to break), and every section's answer is the same as the story's: not that he beats it, but that something
is given.

## Rules of thumb

The full rules for writing, naming and the people are in `docs/writing.md`. The short form:

- He never speaks, and has no name.
- Nobody says grace, faith, sin, salvation, redemption, heaven or God.
- A new name should sound worn, not invented: one or two syllables and a plain title (of the Vigil, the Penitent).
- When in doubt, say less.
